Status: **normative spec for five parallel work packages** (A fair value, B strategy and sizing,
C paper engine and backtest, E integration, F UI). Revision 2, 2026-10-05: resolves every blocker,
major and minor issue raised by the two design reviews of revision 1; each resolution is recorded in
**§14 Design decisions** (D1-D62, with an index from the review issue to its decision). If this
document and a skeleton docstring disagree, this document wins. Do not edit this file while
implementing: list any deviation you had to make, and why, in your final report.

Inputs this spec is built on (read them when a section points to them):
`docs/DESIGN.md` (current design), `docs/STRATEGY.md` (strategy guide, owned by another task: do
not edit), and the research notes in the session scratchpad
(`strategy/mechanics_notes.md`, `strategy/tournament_notes.md`, `strategy/fc_tourn_summary.txt`,
`strategy/tournament_sim_output.txt`, `paper/external_apis.md`, `paper/codebase_map.md`,

external-API fixtures are in `tests/fixtures/external/`.

What changed most since revision 1 (read §14 for the reasons):

* The verdict (§6.9): counts every idea entered (open ones at liquidation value), uses a two-way
  cluster-robust t-interval (races, and 2-hour blocks x party direction), never says more than
  "Promising, not proof" before 48 covered hours on two days, and only ONE pre-registered portfolio
  (the user's sizing policy **at human speed**, `human:<sizing>`) gets the full ladder.
* A signal-level **event study** (§6.14) measures how fast gaps really close: hundreds of
  observations a day instead of a dozen closed trades.
* Sizing (§5.6): buy limits use `floor_tick`, Kelly on the depth-walked average fill, certainty
  shrink, a delta-weighted national-swing cap, and a chaser that sizes a same-party slate as one
  factor bet, keeps `top_k` real, makes at most one bold bet, and estimates the bar linearly.
* Fills and marks (§6.3-6.7): per-portfolio latency, settlements before fills, a known queue for
  at-price maker fills, set entries only on fresh books of every leg, legging-out exits, and marks
  that never price a large position entirely at the touch.
* The backtest (§6.12): preloaded history behind a per-method look-ahead guard, set ideas only from
  same-snapshot ticks, honest fair-value replay, candle fills flagged and excluded, a per-kind
  testability table, an optional GET-only import of outside price history.
* Engineering (§6.15, §7): runtime GET-only guards, a read reserve for the snapshot loop, one lock
  per runner with a published summary, deterministic fast demo runs, a process lease, run
  fingerprints, covered hours, and a separate read-only connection for dashboard backtests.

## 0. Goal, and the rules that bind every package

**The user's goal.** A student in the Susquehanna Predictions Cup (Super Market platform, 2026
midterms, virtual SUSQies, 100,000 start, Cup ends 2026-11-04T17:00:00Z, only the top 3 are paid,
final rank = SUSQies balance after every market resolves) is behind and wants "the most
profitable strategy", and wants the bot to **simulate it for a day on live read-only data** before
they act on it **by hand**. So we build:

1. **Outside fair value** (A): Polymarket, Kalshi and a user-editable file, matched to every Cup
   outcome, with a stated uncertainty. This is the largest legitimate edge the research found.
2. **New idea kinds and goal-based sizing** (B): `value` (Cup price vs fair value), `basket`
   (pair/set arbitrage, including the NO basket when YES bids sum above 1), `hole` (deep resting
   orders that catch liquidity holes), the existing fade/carry/arbitrage with their wrong
   assumptions fixed, and a `chaser` sizing policy next to the conservative one.
3. **A paper trader** (C): several simulated portfolios on the live signals with a conservative
   fill model, liquidation-value marks, a signal-level event study and an honest verdict, judged
   at the speed a person acting by hand can reach; plus a **backtest** that replays stored history
   through the same code.
4. **Integration** (E): the tracker runs fair value and the paper trader in budgeted workers; new
   JSON endpoints; `paper`, `backtest` and `fairvalue` commands; demo scenarios so all of it is
   testable offline and the demo shows a believable simulation within minutes.
5. **A Simulation view** (F) in the dashboard.

**Hard rules (a reviewer will reject any change that breaks one).**

* **Strictly read-only, enforced at runtime** (D20). No code may send any Super Market order or
  write (`POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE` anything, `/orders*`, cancel-all, multi-leg, `POST
  /realtime/token`). New Super Market reads are only: `GET /exchanges/{id}/orderbook`, `GET
  /exchanges/{id}/trades`, `GET /tournaments/{slug}/markets?status=settled`, `GET
  /tournaments/{slug}/leaderboard?limit=100` (all through the existing `SuperMarketClient`). Every
  Super Market client built by `web.build_live`, `web.build_demo` and the `paper` / `fairvalue` /
  `backtest` commands gets `readonly.enforce_get_only` (a request hook that raises
  `ReadOnlyViolation` before a non-GET/HEAD request leaves the process). Outside hosts are read
  with `GET` only through `readonly.outside_client` (anonymous: no `Authorization`/`Cookie` header,
  no credential in the URL; never Polymarket's batch POSTs). The paper trader books simulated orders
  and fills **locally**. The `stream` command keeps its own client (it must mint a realtime token)
  and is not part of the simulator. The opt-in `--llm` attribution judge's POST to Anthropic (its
  own SDK) is outside this rule: it is not a trading venue and is off by default. The only POST
  routes the dashboard serves are its own local ones (`/api/surges/<id>/analyze`, new
  `/api/paper/reset`), which touch nothing outside this process. `tests/test_readonly.py` proves it
  statically (AST) and at runtime (§7.8).
* **No rule-breaking strategies.** No manipulation (no pushing thin prices or the closing VWAP,
  no spoofing), no multi-accounting, no collusion or value transfer, no wash trading. Ideas trade
  on public information, structure (prices that do not add up) and passive liquidity provision.
  Copy for the UI says so where relevant. One process per account (D46): two would double the reads
  on a budget shared by every key.
* **Honest results.** No look-ahead (a decision at `t` uses data known at `t`: stamped `<= t` and,
  for stored tape, fetched by `t`; a fill uses an observation made after the decision plus the
  portfolio's latency); conservative fills (real depth, no re-use of liquidity already taken,
  latency, trade-through or a known queue for passive orders); liquidation-value marks as the
  headline; explicit statistical uncertainty, "not enough evidence yet" below thresholds, and never
  more than "promising, not proof" from one day; demo results labelled as scripted.
* **Python 3.9+** (`from __future__ import annotations`, `typing.List/Dict/Optional`, no `match`,
  no `X | Y` at runtime). No new runtime dependencies (`httpx` only). Offline tests only (no
  network in the test suite).
* **Backward compatible JSON.** Existing endpoint shapes keep every key; new keys are additive.

## 1. Architecture

```
                        +------------------------- Tracker (tracker.py, E) ---------------------------+
 Super Market API  <--- | tracker-loop      every 30 s: market list, bulk prices -> ticks, surges, view |
 (GET only, 90/min      |                   (main client: never gated; 12 reads/min reserved for it)    |
  client limiter,       | tracker-context   every 300 s: constraints, balance, leaderboard(100),        |
  12 reserved for       |                   idea/overround books, settlements (600 s)      [gated]     |
  the loop)             | tracker-backfill / tracker-analysis                                 [gated]     |
                        | tracker-paper     after each cycle: PaperRunner.step()  [own 20/min, 6/min     |
                        |                   while backfill is pending; gated]                           |
                        +-----------------------------------------------^---------------------------------+
 Polymarket / Kalshi <--- tracker-fairvalue every 60 s: FairValueService.refresh()   | (fairvalue.py, A)
 (GET only, anonymous,  (30 s deadline per refresh, own 30/min per host)             |
  own budget)                                                                         |
   PaperRunner.step (paper.py, C)  [one RLock; publishes an immutable summary]        |
     inputs, obs = pipeline.assemble_inputs + pipeline.observation   (E, no reads) ---+
     plan        = engine.wanted_reads(...)            books/tape this step needs (groups all-or-none)
     fulfil      = TrackerMarketReader.book / .trades (E)  <= 8 books + 2 tape pages / step
     signals     = strategy.generate_signals(inputs)   (B)  value/basket/hole/fade/carry/arbitrage
     engine.step = coverage -> settlements -> fills -> marks -> exits -> entries (sizing.py, B)
                   -> event study (study.py, C) -> equity -> persisted in SQLite (store.py v2, E)

   backtest.run_backtest (C) = the same run_step over HistoricalMarket (PreloadedHistory behind a
                               GuardedHistory view; dashboard: separate read-only SQLite connection)

   web.py (E): /api/paper[?run=previous], /api/paper/reset (POST), /api/backtest, /api/fairvalue, + /api/strategy
   web/ (F):   Simulation view, Strategy view additions
```

The snapshot loop is protected by a **read reserve** (D44): every background read (context,
backfill, analysis, paper) waits or is skipped while the shared client limiter has fewer than 12
free slots; the loop's own reads are never gated. Fair value and paper each run in their own
worker; a failing outside host or a slow paper step only shows up as a problem in `/api/status`.

**Lock rule** (D43): nothing calls the paper runner, the engine or the FairValueService while
holding `tracker._lock`. Lock order is always runner lock -> FairValueService lock -> tracker lock
(each taken briefly, never the reverse). `PaperRunner.summary()` and `tracker.status()` read the
runner's published summary without taking the runner lock.

## 3. Conventions and shared types

* **Times** are epoch seconds (UTC floats). ISO only at the edges.
* **YES price vs contract price.** Books, quotes and the tape are YES prices. Orders, fills,
  positions and trades are in **contract prices**: the price of the contract named by `side`
  (`"yes"`: YES price; `"no"`: `1 - YES price`). Buying NO at `q` = selling YES at `1 - q`.
  For a YES best bid `b` and ask `a`: NO ask = `1 - b`, NO bid = `1 - a`.
* **Units** are shares for single-outcome ideas and *sets* (one share of every leg) for set
  ideas (`unit == "sets"`, kinds in `SET_KINDS`).
* **Tick** = 0.005 (`depth.TICK`). `depth.floor_tick(x) = floor(x / 0.005 + 1e-9) * 0.005`,
  `depth.ceil_tick` likewise, rounded to 3 decimals. **Buy limits use `floor_tick`, sell limits
  `ceil_tick`** (a limit never gives away more edge than intended, D8). Prices must lie in
  [0.005, 0.995] for orders (`depth.clamp_price`).
* **Money** is SUSQies; round displayed money to 2 decimals, never round internal accounting.
* **Serialisation**: every dataclass has `to_dict()` (via `models._Serializable`); sets become
  sorted lists, NaN becomes null. Endpoints return `to_dict()` output plus the extra keys of §10.
* **Errors**: providers, the runner and the services never raise on bad data; they report.
  Only shutdown (`tracker._Stopping`), fatal key errors, `readonly.ReadOnlyViolation` and
  `demo.SimClockStall` propagate.
* **Clocks**: every component takes an injected `clock`; nothing in a simulated step reads
  `time.time()` directly (D42).
* **Covered hours**: every "hours run" shown or used for a threshold is *covered* time (§6.11), not
  wall-clock time.

## 4. Package A: outside fair value (`fairvalue.py`)

Research: `paper/external_apis.md` (every field and trap below is documented there with
evidence). Fixtures: `tests/fixtures/external/` (`polymarket_*.json`, `kalshi_*.json` share the

**Mock matching rule (D57).** The test transport answers a request when its path equals the
fixture's path and its query parameters, compared as a **multiset of (name, value) pairs ignoring
`limit` and parameter order**, equal the fixture's; repeated `id=` parameters count individually.
Providers send exactly the parameters this section names (Gamma `/markets?id=..&id=..&limit=n`;
Gamma `/events?tag_slug=midterms&active=true&closed=false&limit=100&offset=k`; Kalshi
`/markets?tickers=a,b&limit=100`), so a fixture keyed `limit=100` matches a request with
`limit=50`. A request no fixture matches gets a 404 from the mock (and the test fails if it was
expected to succeed). A owns the fixtures and adds the missing ones: a 429 with `Retry-After` for
Gamma `/markets` and for Kalshi `/markets`, and a fixture for the history endpoints (§4.11).

All provider HTTP goes through `readonly.outside_client(label=..., transport=..., timeout=...)`
(GET/HEAD only, anonymous, no redirects). Tests pass `transport=httpx.MockTransport(...)`.

### 4.1 Race-key normaliser

`parse_race(title, option=None) -> Optional[RaceRef]`:

| Title (and option) | RaceRef |
|---|---|
| `Will the Democratic Party win the New Hampshire Senate?` | `2026:SENATE:NH`, office SENATE, state NH, party D |
| `Will the Republican Party win the Georgia Governor?` | `2026:GOVERNOR:GA`, R |
| `Will the Democratic Party win the AZ-01 House race?` | `2026:HOUSE:AZ-01`, office HOUSE, state AZ, district `AZ-01`, D |
| `Will the Independent Party win the Nebraska Senate?` | `2026:SENATE:NE`, I |
| `Will the Democratic Party win the U.S. Senate?` | `2026:SENATE_CONTROL:US`, D |
| `Will the Republican Party win the U.S. House?` | `2026:HOUSE_CONTROL:US`, R |
| `Will Democrats win the Michigan Senate race?` (demo) | `2026:SENATE:MI`, D |
| `Will Republicans win Ohio House District 9?` (demo) | `2026:HOUSE:OH-09`, R |
| `Which party will control the Senate after the midterms?` + option `Republicans` | `2026:SENATE_CONTROL:US`, R, source "option" |
| `Which party will control the House after the midterms?` + `Democrats` | `2026:HOUSE_CONTROL:US`, D, source "option" |
| `Who will win the Arizona Governor race?` + `Any other candidate` | `2026:GOVERNOR:AZ`, O, source "option" |
| `Will Republicans keep control of the Pennsylvania Senate?` | None (a state chamber) |
| `How many House seats will Democrats win?`, `Will a Libertarian candidate win a U.S. House seat?`, debate markets | None |

Rules: regexes `CUP_TITLE_RE`, `HOUSE_RACE_RE`, `LEGACY_TITLE_RE`, `LEGACY_DISTRICT_RE` in the
skeleton; state names via `STATE_CODES` (exact, case-sensitive full names; "U.S." handled
first); districts zero-padded (`OH-9` -> `OH-09`); parties via `PARTY_WORDS` and `party_of`,
which also reads trailing tags like `"Chris Pappas (D)"`; titles are stripped and internal
whitespace collapsed first. The year prefix is always `2026`. Anything else returns None.
Acceptance: every title in `supermarket_titles.txt` parses to its `race_key` and `party` column.

`races_for(infos, overrides)` applies `parse_race(info.market_title, info.option)` to every
outcome; a user override (`fair_value_map.json` `overrides[eid]` with `race_key` and `party`)
replaces it with `source="override"`. `races_for` is pure (no I/O) and is used even when fair
value is off (baskets need race groups).

### 4.2 Pins and targets

`fv_pins.PINS` (237 rows, generated from the scout's table; columns in `PIN_COLUMNS`): keyed by
`(race_key, party)` -> Polymarket market id (231 rows), Kalshi ticker (220; the two wrong KY pins
are blanked, §4.5), `match_kind`
(`NEAR` for chamber control and Independent legs, else `EXACT`). `pins_by_race()` indexes them.
`build_targets(infos, races, overrides)` makes one `MatchTarget` per outcome with
`pins = {"polymarket": id, "kalshi": ticker}` (omit empty ones); an override's `polymarket` /
`kalshi` value replaces the pin, an explicit `null` removes it, `disabled: true` sets `disabled`,
`trade_near: true` sets `trade_near`, `confirmed: true` sets `confirmed`. Outcomes without a race
get no pins (manual entries can still price them). `known_unmatched_races()` = race keys whose
every pin row has neither id (the Alaska Governor today): discovery never searches for them, and
their rows say "No outside market prices this race by party (Alaska Governor)".
**Pins are candidates, not truth**: a provider uses a pinned id only after validating it against
live data (§4.4, §4.5) and drops it otherwise; pins come from other students' files (D50).

### 4.3 Manual fair values (`data/<slug>/fair_values.json` or `.csv`)

The directory is the tracker's data folder (`settings.data_dir / context.label`, demo:
`<data dir>/demo`). JSON wins when both files exist.

```json
{"version": 1,
 "values": [
   {"exchange_id": "1070", "probability": 0.55, "uncertainty": 0.03, "source": "Silver Bulletin", "note": "model 57%, shaded", "updated_at": "2026-10-04T12:00:00Z"},
   {"race_key": "2026:SENATE:TX", "party": "D", "probability": "58%", "source": "my model"},
   {"match": "Maine Senate", "party": "D", "probability": 0.52},
   {"match": "re:^Will the Republican Party win the Iowa Senate\\?$", "probability": 0.6}
 ]}
```

CSV: a header row is required; columns (any subset, any order) `exchange_id, race_key, party,
match, probability, uncertainty, source, note, updated_at`; `#` lines are comments.

* `probability` / `uncertainty`: `parse_probability` (0..1 as is; `"55%"` -> 0.55; numbers in
  (1, 100] -> /100; empty/null = template row, ignored; anything else is an error naming the
  line). A missing `uncertainty` is `MANUAL_UNCERTAINTY` (0.02): your own numbers are estimates too.
* `updated_at`: ISO or epoch; missing -> the file's mtime. Older than `MANUAL_MAX_AGE_S` (72 h)
  -> `usable=False`, reason "manual value is older than 72 h; update updated_at".
* Resolution per outcome: `exchange_id` > `race_key`+`party` > `match` (+ optional `party`). A
  `match` that selects more than one outcome (after the party filter) is an error
  ("ambiguous: matches 3 outcomes") and is skipped. Later entries of the same precedence override
  earlier ones (and that is reported as a warning in `errors`).
* Result: `FairValue(source="manual", match_kind="MANUAL", confidence="medium",
  match_confidence=1.0, usable=True, value=p, uncertainty=u, note, manual_updated_at,
  as_of=updated_at)`. Manual values are never "suspect" (the user typed them) and are not
  renormalised per race unless every leg of the race is manual (§4.7 step 5).
* `load()` re-reads only when the mtime changed, never raises, and lists every problem
  (unreadable JSON, unknown keys, bad numbers, unknown exchange ids) in `ManualLoad.errors`.
* `template(targets)` returns a starter document with every outcome and `probability: null`
  (the CLI writes it with `fairvalue --template`, never overwriting an existing file).

### 4.4 Polymarket provider (Gamma, `https://gamma-api.polymarket.com`, no auth)

* **Steady state** (every refresh): `GET /markets?id=<id1>&id=<id2>...&limit=<n>` with at most
  `POLYMARKET_IDS_PER_CALL` (50) ids per call (always pass `limit` = number of ids). About 5
  calls for the 231 pinned ids. The response is a **bare JSON array** of market objects.
* **Validation of a pin** (on its first successful read, then every `DISCOVERY_EVERY_S`), all of:
  `active == true`; `closed == false`; an `endDate` (or `endDateIso`) in 2026; the parent event
  (`events[0].title`, when present) does not match `REJECT_EVENT_RE`; **the race is named**:
  `race_named_in(events[0].title, race)` or `race_named_in(question, race)` (state full name + office
  word, `ST-NN`/`ST-N` for House seats, "Senate"/"House" for chamber control, D50); **YES is index 0**:
  `json.loads(outcomes) == ["Yes", "No"]` (tolerate an already-decoded list); and `groupItemTitle`
  carries the target's party (`party_of` of a `(D)`/`(R)`/`(I)` tag, or exactly `Democrat`,
  `Republican`, `Democratic Party`, `Republican Party`, `Independent`). Never validate on the slug
  (it is wrong for ME-02 and Montana). Failure drops the pin with a reason naming the failed check
  ("pinned Polymarket market 630844 names New Hampshire, not Maine: dropped"); the outcome then has
  no Polymarket quote.
* **Discovery** for races without a valid pin and not in `known_unmatched_races()`, at most every
  `DISCOVERY_EVERY_S` (6 h): `GET /events?tag_slug=midterms&active=true&closed=false&limit=100&offset=k`
  (`DISCOVERY_PAGE_LIMIT`; pages until a short page, at most 3). Accept an event only if its title
  matches the race (`"<State> Senate Election Winner"`, `"<State> Governor Election Winner"`,
  `"<ST>-<NN> House Election Winner"`, or for chamber control the
  `which-party-will-win-the-senate|house-in-2026` slugs), does not match `REJECT_EVENT_RE`, has an
  `endDate` in 2026, is unique, and has exactly one active market whose `groupItemTitle` carries the
  party and whose `outcomes` are `["Yes", "No"]`. Discovered matches get `confidence =
  CONF_DISCOVERY` (0.85), `source="discovery"`.
* **Parsing** (every variant the scout found): `outcomes`, `outcomePrices`, `clobTokenIds` are
  **JSON-encoded strings** (`json.loads`; tolerate already-decoded lists); `bestBid`, `bestAsk`,
  `lastTradePrice`, `spread`, `volumeNum`, `liquidityNum` are numbers but accept numeric strings;
  `volume` / `liquidity` are strings. Prices: `bid=bestBid`, `ask=bestAsk`, `last=lastTradePrice`;
  if both bid and ask are missing use `outcomePrices[0]` as `last` with flag "last-trade-only".
  Placeholders (`active == false`, or bid 0 / ask 1) are dropped with flag "placeholder". There is
  no quote timestamp: `fetched_at` = our clock, `venue_updated_at` = `updatedAt` (informational).
  Keep `clobTokenIds[0]` (the YES token) on the match for the history import.
* **Errors, timing and budget** (D56): one `outside_client` per provider, timeout
  `EXTERNAL_TIMEOUT_S` (8 s), a `SlidingWindowLimiter(EXTERNAL_READS_PER_MIN=30)` of its own (it
  never touches the Super Market client or its limiter). `refresh(targets, now, deadline=)` starts
  no request after `deadline` (the service passes `now + REFRESH_DEADLINE_S`, 30 s, for all
  providers together, so a black-holed network costs at most ~38 s per refresh, not 88 s). **The
  first** connection error, proxy refusal (`403` at CONNECT surfaces as `httpx.ProxyError` /
  `ConnectError`), DNS failure or timeout ends this provider's refresh at once with status
  `"offline"` (no further calls that refresh). HTTP 429 -> honour `Retry-After` capped at
  `MAX_RETRY_AFTER_S` (600 s; else the backoff) as `next_try_at`; 5xx -> `"error"`. Consecutive
  failures back off through `BACKOFF_S` (30, 60, 120, 300, 600 s) via `next_try_at`; a refresh
  during backoff returns status `"backoff"` with no requests. Error texts are one sentence, e.g.
  "Polymarket is unreachable from this machine (connection refused by the network proxy): no
  outside fair value from Polymarket." Never fake a value.
* Do not use `GET /price` (side semantics conflict) or the POST batch endpoints.

### 4.5 Kalshi provider (`https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2`, no auth for reads)

* Steady state: `GET /markets?tickers=<t1,t2,...>&limit=100` (comma-separated, at most 100 per
  call): about 3 calls for the 222 pinned tickers. Response `{"markets": [...], "cursor": "..."}`.
* Validation, all of: `status` in {`active`, `open`} (the response says `active` while the filter
  says `open`); the event ticker ends in `-26`, `-2026` or `-26NOV`; **the ticker encodes the race**:

* Events (discovery, optional): `GET /events?status=open&with_nested_markets=true&limit=200&cursor=`;

* Base URL fallback: on a connection error to `KALSHI_BASE_URLS[0]` try `[1]` once for that refresh

### 4.6 Matches, overrides and the persisted map (`data/<slug>/fair_value_map.json`)

```json
{"version": 1,
 "updated_at": "2026-10-04T21:00:00Z",
 "overrides": {
   "1070": {"polymarket": "630844", "kalshi": "SENATENH-26-D", "race_key": "2026:SENATE:NH", "party": "D", "confirmed": true, "note": "checked by hand"},
   "842": {"trade_near": true, "note": "I accept Polymarket's control wording"},
   "1057": {"disabled": true, "note": "no outside market for Alaska Governor"}
 },
 "matches": {
   "1070": [{"venue": "polymarket", "external_id": "630844", "label": "Chris Pappas (D) - New Hampshire Senate Election Winner",
             "kind": "EXACT", "confidence": 0.95, "reason": "...", "source": "pin", "validated_at": 1791100000.0}]
 }}
```

* `overrides` are written only by the user; allowed keys `polymarket`, `kalshi`, `race_key`, `party`,
  `disabled`, `trade_near`, `confirmed`, `note` (anything else is listed in `status()["errors"]`).
  `matches` are written by the service after each successful validation/discovery (atomic: write a
  temp file in the same folder, then `os.replace`; re-read the file first and keep `overrides`
  verbatim, so a user edit made meanwhile is never lost). A missing or unreadable file is treated as
  empty (and reported once in `status()["errors"]`).
* **Reload** (D51): `MatchMap.reload_if_changed()` runs at the start of every `refresh` and every
  `set_targets`; when the mtime changed, overrides are re-read and the targets rebuilt, so **an edit
  takes effect at the next refresh** (within 60 s). `status()` = `{"path", "exists", "overrides":
  count, "loaded_at", "errors"}` and is part of `FairValueService.status()` and `/api/fairvalue`.
* **How the user sees and fixes a wrong match** (D51): every `/api/fairvalue` row lists each venue
  match (external id, label, kind, confidence, reason) and copyable text snippets from
  `override_snippet(target, action)`: `disable` (`"1070": {"disabled": true, "note": "wrong match"}`),
  `pin` (`"1070": {"polymarket": "630844", "kalshi": "SENATENH-26-D", "confirmed": true}`), and, when
  relevant, `confirm` (suspect matches) and `trade_near` (NEAR matches). The CLI prints the same with
  `fairvalue --show-override EID`. The README explains where the file is and that the change applies
  within a minute.
* Confidence: override 1.0; validated pin `CONF_PIN_EXACT` 0.95 (`EXACT`) or `CONF_PIN_NEAR`
  0.80 (`NEAR`); discovery 0.85; a quote whose match confidence is below
  `MIN_MATCH_CONFIDENCE` (0.8) is never used for a fair value.
* `reason` is one plain sentence a human can check, e.g. "Pinned Polymarket market 630844
  (participant map), validated live: active, ends 2026-11-04, names the New Hampshire Senate,
  'Chris Pappas (D)' is the Democratic leg."

### 4.7 Combining quotes into one fair value

1. Per venue quote: `venue_value(bid, ask, last)` (mid when `0 < bid < ask < 1` and spread <=
   0.10; else last trade with "last-trade-only"; else None) -> `raw_value`.
2. `normalise_race`: per venue and race key, over the legs that venue **quoted** for that race (a
   leg is quoted when it has a `raw_value` after placeholder filtering: the NE Senate "Democrat"
   placeholder leg is not quoted and does not poison the sum): if every quoted leg has a
   `raw_value` and the sum is within `RACE_SUM_BAND` (0.95..1.05), `value = raw_value / sum`;
   otherwise `value = None` with "suspect-sum" for all those legs. One quoted leg alone keeps
   `value = raw_value`.
3. `blend(eid, quotes, now, race=, cup_mid=, trade_near=, confirmed=)`: drop quotes older than
   `FV_MAX_AGE_S` (**90 s**, flag "stale") or below the match threshold; if none remain ->
   `FairValue(value=None, usable=False, reason=...)`. Otherwise blend in log-odds (clip each value to
   [0.01, 0.99]) with weights `1 / max(spread / 2, 0.005) ** 2` (last-trade-only quotes use spread
   0.10). `agreement` = max - min of the venue values (None with one). Confidence: `high` if some
   venue has spread <= 0.04 and (one venue, or agreement <= 0.03); `medium` if agreement <= 0.05;
   else `low` with `usable=False` ("Polymarket 0.58 and Kalshi 0.49 disagree by 9 cents: not traded
   on"). `bid`/`ask` = the tightest venue's. `as_of` = the oldest `fetched_at` used. `source` = the
   venue, or `"blend"`. **`uncertainty = uncertainty_of(quotes used, agreement)`** = max(agreement,
   the tightest venue's half-spread, the largest `FEE_BAND` of the venues used: Polymarket 0.01,
   Kalshi 0.02, demo 0) (D17). **NEAR matches are display-only** (D17): `usable=False`, reason "Near
   match (different settlement wording): shown, not traded", unless the override sets `trade_near`
   (then usable, confidence capped at medium, and the strategy adds `near_extra_edge`).
   **Suspect gap** (D50): when `cup_mid` is known, the match is not `confirmed` and `|value -
   cup_mid| > SUSPECT_GAP` (0.25): `suspect=True`, `usable=False`, reason "Suspect match: outside
   0.86 vs Cup 0.42; check the match (or confirm it in fair_value_map.json)".
4. `combine(manual, external)`: a manual value replaces the external one for that outcome;
   the external quotes stay in `sources`, and `agreement` = |manual - external value|.
5. `renormalise_combined(values, races)` (D38): for every race whose legs ALL have a value: if their
   sum is within `RACE_SUM_BAND`, rescale them to sum to 1 (the D and R legs may come from
   different venue sets); otherwise every leg of the race becomes unusable ("legs disagree: the
   race's fair values sum to 1.08"). A race with a missing leg is left as is.
6. `prev_value` / `prev_as_of`: the outcome's value and `as_of` at the previous refresh (the
   strategy's jump check, §5.2).

Fair values are **not** shrunk here (no favourite-longshot adjustment): that is a strategy
decision (B, `longshot_shrink`).

### 4.8 `FairValueService` (what the tracker owns)

* `FairValueService(directory, mode="auto"|"manual"|"off", providers, manual, recorder,
  refresh_recorder, clock, cup_mids)`. `mode="off"`: `current().enabled == False`, no files read,
  no HTTP. `"manual"`: only `ManualFairValues`. `"auto"`: manual + `default_providers("auto")`
  (`[PolymarketProvider(), KalshiProvider()]`). The demo passes
  `providers=[demo.DemoFairValueProvider(market)]` instead. `cup_mids()` returns the Cup's current
  YES mids (the tracker passes a getter over its last published snapshot; it must not take
  `tracker._lock`).
* `set_targets(infos)` after each market-list refresh (open outcomes only; the tracker calls it
  after releasing its lock); reloads the map if changed; computes races (`races_for` with the map's
  overrides) and targets.
* `refresh(now)`: `map.reload_if_changed()`; `manual.load()`; each provider
  `refresh(targets, now, deadline=now + REFRESH_DEADLINE_S)` (sequentially); normalise, blend,
  combine, renormalise; record; keep the snapshot. Never raises (an unexpected exception becomes
  `snapshot.errors` and a log warning).
* **Recording** (for replays, D24, D59): call `recorder(records)` with one `FairValueRecord` per
  outcome whose value, bid or ask moved by at least `FV_RECORD_MIN_CHANGE` (one tick), or whose
  `usable`, `confidence` or `suspect` changed, since its last record, plus a heartbeat record for
  unchanged ones every `FV_RECORD_HEARTBEAT_S` (600 s). A record carries `as_of` (the live
  FairValue.as_of), `venues` (venues whose quotes were used) and a compact `detail`
  `{"as_of", "venues", "match_kind", "match_confidence", "uncertainty", "prev_value", "suspect"}`
  (no reason text, no `sources`). After every refresh also call
  `refresh_recorder(FairValueRefresh(ts=now, venues={venue: {"status", "fetched_at"}}))` (one row
  per refresh: it proves that unchanged values were re-confirmed, so the replay knows their real
  age). Recorder exceptions are caught.
* `current()` is a deep-enough copy (callers may not mutate shared state); `races()`,
  `targets()`, `matches()` (one `MatchRow` per target), `status()` =
  `{"mode", "enabled", "last_refresh_at", "usable", "total", "providers": {name: {"status",
  "last_ok_at", "last_error", "requests", "matched", "quoted", "next_try_at"}}, "manual":
  {"path", "exists", "entries", "errors"}, "map": MatchMap.status()}`.
* Thread-safety: `refresh` runs in the tracker's fair-value worker; `current`/`status`/`matches`
  are called from the paper worker and HTTP threads: guard state with one lock, never hold it
  during HTTP or while calling `cup_mids`.

## 5. Package B: idea kinds and sizing (`strategy.py`, `sizing.py`)

### 5.1 `generate_signals(inputs, params=None) -> List[Opportunity]`

Every idea of every kind, **unsized** (`suggested_shares = 0`, `suggested_cost = 0`,
`sizing = None`), deterministic (sorted by `score` desc, then `idea_id`). It replaces the
inline idea assembly of `build_report`; `build_report` calls it and then sizes (§5.7).

Common rules for every idea it returns:

* Only open outcomes (`inputs.infos`) with a two-sided quote in `inputs.latest`. The strategy
  does not judge quote age itself: the builders put only *current* quotes in `latest` (the live
  assembler leaves out view rows flagged `stale`; the replay adapter leaves out ticks older than
  `2 x step_s`), so an outcome missing from `latest` simply yields no trade idea. `priced_from` =
  the quote's `PricePoint.source` ("tick", or "candle" in replays).
* Fields always set: `idea_id` (`idea_id_for`), `race_key` (from `inputs.races`), `bet`,
  `bet_limit`, `exit_plan`, `order_type`, `limit_price`, `levels`, `max_units` (None when no usable
  book), `factor_delta`, `called_prob` (when used), `fv_uncertainty` (when a fair value or a kind
  default was used), `settlement_regime = inputs.settlement_regime`, `settles_before_cup_end`
  (`settles_before(..., margin_s=params.settle_margin_s)`), `horizon_hours`, `rationale`, `risks`,
  `confidence`, `score`; plus `fair_value` / `fair_source` whenever a fair value was used.
* `idea_id_for` formats: single `"<kind>:x<eid>:<side>"` (+ `":s<surge_id>"` for fades, +
  `":<extra>"` if needed); sets `"<kind>:set:<eid1>+<eid2>+...:<side1><side2>..."` with legs
  sorted by exchange id (sides as `y`/`n`). The same idea in the next build has the same id.
* **`limit_price`** (taker, D8): buy limits use `depth.floor_tick`, so a limit never gives away more
  than half the edge: single ideas `floor_tick(entry + 0.5 * edge_per_share)` (never below the
  entry, never above the contract's fair value); value ideas use `value_limit` (§5.2: the highest
  such tick whose MARGINAL edge still clears the required edge); sets: each leg
  `limit = floor_tick(leg price + 0.5 * set_edge / n_legs)`, and `limit_price` = their sum (bids
  0.60/0.45: NO legs 0.40 -> 0.41 and 0.55 -> 0.56, set limit 0.97, keeping 0.03 of a 0.05 edge).
* **`levels`** and `max_units`: from the stored book (`inputs.latest[eid].book`, at most
  `params.book_max_age_s` old and consistent with the touch, i.e. the existing `_book_levels`
  rule), `depth.contract_levels(bids, asks, side, "buy")` cut at `limit_price`; sets:
  `depth.set_levels(leg levels each cut at its leg limit, limit_price)`. `max_units` = the units in
  `levels` (None and `levels = []` when no such book exists).
* `bet` per kind, priced at the decision touch: binary for value/carry (`p = entry + ev`, `gain = 1 -
  entry`, `loss = entry`, `cost = entry`), bracket for fade (`p`, `gain = target_bid - entry`, `loss
  = entry - stop_bid`, `cost = entry`), riskless or bounded for basket/arbitrage (§5.3), fixed for
  holes (`cap_pct = params.hole_max_stake_pct`, `p` = hole_fill_prob, `gain = target_bid - price`,
  `loss = price`, `cost = price`). `bet_limit` = `sizing.bet_at_cost(bet, limit_price)` (what the
  idea is worth if the whole order fills at its limit; shown on the card).
* **`factor_delta`** (D10): `strategy.factor_delta(race, side, p_yes, params)` per share for single
  ideas (SUSQies gained for a 1-point national swing toward Democrats; 0 for parties other than D/R
  and outcomes without a race); 0 for set ideas.
* **`score`** (D33) for every trade kind = `growth_score(bet, edge, confidence, entry)`: the expected
  profit in percent of equity at the conservative capped size, times confidence. It ranks a 0.50
  toss-up with a 5-cent edge (0.25) above a 0.10 longshot with a 2-cent edge (0.11); the old
  `edge / entry x confidence` ranked them the other way (0.10 vs 0.20). `watch` keeps 0. Risk-mode
  multipliers still apply in `build_report`. B updates the legacy assertions that check
  `score == expected_return * confidence` (test_strategy.py lines ~204, 288, 380) and lists them.
* Conflicts (`_resolve_conflicts`, extended): one net position per exchange, so a later idea that
  buys the opposite contract of an earlier (higher-score) idea's leg on the same exchange gets a
  risk line "Conflicts with ..."; a carry opposite an arbitrage/basket leg is dropped as before.
  When an engine-violation `arbitrage` idea buys exactly the legs and sides of a `basket` idea,
  emit only the basket (it carries the exit plan). **Two value ideas on different legs of one race
  that bet on different winners** (YES on two legs, or NO on two legs) are an unbalanced partial set
  priced on possibly inconsistent fair values: keep only the higher-score one (D38).
* `watch` ideas keep `order_type = "taker"`, `bet = None`, `exit_plan = None` and are never traded.

### 5.2 `value` ideas (`value_opportunity`)

For every open outcome with a fair value `fv` where `fv.usable`, `fv.value is not None`, `not
fv.suspect`, and `now - fv.as_of <= params.fv_max_age_s` (90 s; manual: `manual_fv_max_age_s`;
imported history in replays: `history_fv_max_age_s`):

* Quote: YES bid `b`, ask `a` (both required); `half = (a - b) / 2`; YES mid `m = (a + b) / 2`.
* **Freshness checks** (D18; external values only, not manual ones):
  * *jumping outside price*: skip when `fv.prev_value` is known and `|fv.value - fv.prev_value| >
    params.fv_jump_fraction * |fv.value - m|` ("the outside price moved by 4 cents in the last
    minute: waiting for it to settle");
  * *the Cup moved since the outside price was read*: with `m_then` = the newest
    `inputs.recent_mids[eid]` entry at or before `fv.as_of` (skip the check when none), skip when
    `|m - m_then| > params.cup_move_fraction * |fv.value - m|` **and** the Cup moved away from the fair
    value (`|fv.value - m| > |fv.value - m_then|`) ("the Cup moved 8 cents since the outside price
    was read: it may already reflect newer news"). This is what a stale fair value looks like when
    faster bots anchored to Polymarket have already moved the Cup.
* For each side: `entry` = `a` (YES) or `1 - b` (NO); `q` = `fv.value` (YES) or `1 - fv.value`
  (NO); `contract_mid` = `m` or `1 - m`; if `q < 0.15`, `q = q * (1 - params.longshot_shrink)`
  (favourite-longshot bias, Page & Clemen; say so in the rationale).
* `resolution_gap = q - entry` (EV if held to a 1/0 resolution).
* `target_bid = floor_tick(q - half - params.value_exit_buffer)`; the exit order's limit is
  `target_bid - 0.005` (§6.5), so **`convergence_gain = target_bid - 0.005 - entry`** (what selling on
  convergence banks, exit spread and exit tick included, D34).
* `called = called_prob(race, max(fv.value, 1 - fv.value), params)` (other kinds pass the Cup
  mid's favourite side, `max(mid, 1 - mid)`); `ev, regime_text, detail = regime_ev(q, entry,
  contract_mid, regime, called, params, settles_before_end=...)` (D16):
  * `resolved_outcomes`, or the market settles before the Cup end: `ev = q - entry`;
  * `vwap_closeout`: with `q_u = 0.5 + (q - 0.5) * uncalled_shrink` (an uncalled race is a close
    race), `q_c = clip((q - (1 - called) * q_u) / called, 0, 1)` (the branches average to `q`) and
    `g = q - contract_mid` (the Cup's mispricing, of which `1 - unconverged_share` persists into an
    uncalled race's closeout VWAP): `ev = called * (q_c - entry) + (1 - called) * (q_u - (1 -
    unconverged_share) * g - entry)`. The uncalled branch of a favourite is a **loss** (a 0.97
    favourite still uncalled closes near 0.6). The rationale calls this "a heuristic for how an
    uncalled race closes" and states both branch payoffs;
  * `unknown`: `min(resolved ev, vwap ev)` (conservative).
* **Required edge** (D17): `required = params.min_value_edge * (params.longshot_min_edge_mult if q <
  0.15 else 1) + fv.uncertainty (0 when None) + (params.near_extra_edge if fv.match_kind == "NEAR"
  else 0)`. Keep the side whose `ev` is larger; emit only if `convergence_gain >= required` **and**
  `ev >= required`, and `value_limit(...)` is not None. Otherwise None.
* `limit_price = value_limit(q, entry, contract_mid, target_bid - 0.005, regime, called, required,
  params)`: the highest tick `L` in `[entry, floor_tick(entry + 0.5 * ev)]` whose marginal
  `regime_ev` at `L` and `target_bid - 0.005 - L` are both `>= required` (every share the order can
  fill still clears the bar).
* `prob_win = q`, `edge = ev`, `expected_return = ev / entry`, `target_price = target_bid`,
  `stop_price = None`, `fv_uncertainty = fv.uncertainty`, confidence = {high: 0.8, medium: 0.6,
  low: 0.4} for external, 0.6 for manual/demo, times `fv.match_confidence`; `score` = growth score.
* `exit_plan = ExitPlan(kind="value", target_bid=target_bid, dynamic_fv_target=True,
  exit_buffer=params.value_exit_buffer, hold_to_resolution=(regime == "resolved_outcomes"),
  exit_before_ts=<regime rule below>, note="Sell when the YES bid reaches 0.570 (fair value 0.58
  less half the spread and 0.005); otherwise hold")`.
* **Regime exit rule** (all kinds): `exit_before_ts = cup_end - params.closeout_buffer_s` when
  the regime is `vwap_closeout`, or when it is `unknown` and the race's state is in
  `SLOW_COUNT_STATES`; else None. Markets settling before the Cup end never get one. (A position
  whose EV leans on the uncalled branch is therefore flat before the closeout window.)
* `called_prob(race, fav, params)`: `fav >= 0.95` -> `called_prob_safe` (0.97; 0.80 if the state
  is in `RCV_STATES`); state in `SLOW_COUNT_STATES` -> `called_prob_slow` (0.40); known race
  elsewhere -> `called_prob_fast` (0.85); no race -> the mean of fast and slow.
* Rationale (examples of the required content, wording free): fair value with source,
  confidence, uncertainty and age; the gap at the ask and the required edge; the exit target and
  what it banks; the regime sentence from `regime_ev`; depth at the limit when known. Risks:
  outside prices can be wrong (biases, fees, different resolution rules: Polymarket media
  calls/certification, Kalshi swearing-in); faster bots close gaps first, and a gap that closes
  because the outside price moved means our fair value was stale; under a VWAP closeout an
  uncalled race closes near a coin flip.

**Hand example** (exact numbers, `arch2/spec_numbers.py`): ask 0.52, bid 0.51, fv 0.58 from one
venue with uncertainty 0.01, a fast-count state (called 0.85), regime `unknown`: target_bid 0.570,
exit limit 0.565, convergence gain 0.045, resolution gap 0.060; vwap branch `q_c` 0.58988 (called
payoff +0.06988), `q_u` 0.524 (uncalled payoff -0.0415), vwap ev 0.053175 = ev; required 0.03; top
tick `floor_tick(0.5466)` = 0.545 fails (marginal ev 0.028175), so `limit_price` = 0.535 (marginal ev
0.038); with uncertainty 0 (required 0.02) the limit is 0.545. Conservative sizing on equity
100,000: quarter-Kelly at the touch 5,326 shares (2,769.52 SUSQies); with the certainty factor
0.9658 (sigma 0.01) 5,144; on a book with asks 0.52 x 2,000, 0.53 x 3,000, 0.535 x 5,000 the
depth-walked rule gives 4,601 shares at an expected average 0.52565.

### 5.3 `basket` ideas (`group_races`, `basket_opportunities`)

**Groups** (`group_races(infos, races, constraints)`):

* *Race groups*: open outcomes sharing a `race_key` with **different parties**, at least two
  (the Cup's D/R pairs, D/R/I triples). Not exhaustive (an unlisted winner makes every leg NO).
* *Market groups*: all open outcomes of one multi-outcome market (`MarketInfo.is_multi`, i.e.
  `ExchangeInfo`s sharing a `market_id`), at least two. Exhaustive only if an option matches
  `CATCH_ALL_RE` ("Any other candidate") or an engine relationship of type `complementary`
  covers every member (`constraints["data"][i]["relationshipType"]`/`"type"`, members via
  `_members`). `why_exhaustive` says which.
* Deduplicate by member set (a market group whose members are also a race group is one group,
  source "market").

**Same-snapshot quotes** (D53): every leg needs a `PricePoint` with `source == "tick"`, and the
legs' quote timestamps may differ by at most `params.same_snapshot_s` (5 s: one bulk read is
chunked into a few requests). Candle-based or mismatched quotes never produce a set idea (two
legs' closes from different hours can sum to 1.05 without any real arbitrage). Each leg dict
carries `quote_ts`.

**NO basket** (any group): every leg needs a YES bid `b_i`. `S_b = sum(b_i)`. If `S_b - 1 >=
basket_min_edge` (0.01): buy NO on every leg at `1 - b_i`; set cost `C = n - S_b`; guaranteed
payoff at a 1/0 settlement `floor = n - 1` (exactly `n - 1` if exhaustive, at least `n - 1`
otherwise); `edge = floor - C = S_b - 1` per set.

**YES basket** (exhaustive groups only): every leg needs a YES ask `a_i`; `S_a = sum(a_i)`; if
`1 - S_a >= basket_min_edge`: buy YES on every leg; `C = S_a`, `floor = 1`, `edge = 1 - S_a`.
For non-exhaustive groups never emit it (it is not riskless).

**Bet: riskless only when it is** (D27). A set pays its floor only if every leg settles 1/0. Under a
VWAP closeout an uncalled race cashes each leg at its own VWAP (payoff `2 - (VWAP_D + VWAP_R)`: zero
profit if the over-round persists, less if it widens), and a REFUND or ruling on one leg can turn a
set into a large loss (bids 0.60/0.45: if the D market is refunded and R wins, the set returns 0.40
for 0.95). So:

* `bet = BetShape("riskless", p=1, gain=edge, loss=0, cost=C, floor)` only when the regime is
  `resolved_outcomes`, or every leg's race is a fast count (`called_prob >= called_prob_fast` and
  the state is not in `SLOW_COUNT_STATES`);
* otherwise `bet = BetShape("bounded", p=1 - basket_refund_prob, gain=edge * called, loss=C -
  min_i(c_i), cost=C, floor, tail_prob=basket_refund_prob)` with `called` the race's called
  probability (the zero-profit uncalled branch is priced as no gain) and `c_i` the legs' contract
  costs (the refund scenario: the cheapest leg refunded, the others lose).

**Depth**: `levels = depth.set_levels(...)`, `max_units` = their total, from stored books; empty /
None if any leg lacks a usable book (the paper engine then defers the entry until every leg has a
fresh book, §6.3).

**Exit plan**: `ExitPlan(kind="basket", min_set_profit=max(basket_exit_min_profit,
basket_exit_fraction * edge), hold_to_resolution=(regime == "resolved_outcomes"),
exit_before_ts=<regime rule>, note="Sell the set when selling every leg brings at least
+0.025 per set over its cost (for a NO basket: entry bid sum minus exit ask sum); otherwise
hold to settlement")`. For a NO basket the exit proceeds per set are `sum(1 - a_i_now)`, so
`profit = S_b(entry) - S_a(now)`; the engine evaluates it on depth-walked proceeds (§6.5).

**Reporting**: `unit="sets"`, `legs` (shape in §3), `entry_price = C`, `edge`, `expected_return
= edge / C`, `prob_win = 1 - tail` (1.0 when riskless), `confidence` 0.9 (race group) / 0.95
(exhaustive market group), `profit_per_capital_day = edge / (C * max(1/24, days_until_settlement))`
with `days_until_settlement` from the latest leg settlement date (Cup markets: the Cup end), `score`
= growth score. Rationale: the bid (or ask) sum, the per-set profit in SUSQies, the per-day return
on capital, the exit rule, "pays even if a third candidate wins (then n NO shares pay)" for NO
baskets, whether it is riskless or bounded and why, and "right after entry the set is worth less at
liquidation than its cost (you would sell into the bids: about 0.93 per set against 0.95 here);
that is the spread, not a loss of the locked edge". Risks: legging (one leg fills, the other
moves), capital locked until the exit or settlement, under a VWAP closeout each leg is cashed
separately (not locked), a tournament ruling/REFUND on one leg.

**Hand example**: bids 0.60 + 0.45 (NV, a slow-count state, regime unknown): C 0.95, edge 0.05,
leg limits 0.41 / 0.56 (set limit 0.97), bounded bet p 0.99, gain 0.02 (called 0.40), loss 0.55;
conservative: 8% cap -> 8,421 sets (quarter-Kelly would allow 32,499); chaser: 20% cap -> 21,052 sets.

### 5.4 `hole` ideas (`hole_candidates`, `hole_opportunities`)

Passive resting buy orders far below a liquid favourite, to catch a careless market order that
sweeps a thin book ("liquidity hole"). Legitimate liquidity provision; never a spoof (each order
is meant to fill, and is small).

* Eligible outcome: two-sided quote with `ask - bid <= hole_max_spread` (0.02); favourite side
  mid >= `hole_min_mid` (0.70) (YES mid >= 0.70 -> buy YES; YES mid <= 0.30 -> buy NO); no
  open surge of any verdict on it; a stored book at most `book_max_age_s` old whose favourite-
  side best bid quantity (YES bids for YES; YES asks for NO) is >= `hole_min_touch_qty` (200).
  **Survival** (D55): an outcome in `inputs.resting_exchanges` keeps its idea without a fresh book
  while its last stored book is at most `2 * book_max_age_s` old and the quote still meets the
  spread and mid rules (so a skipped candidate read does not cancel a resting order and lose its
  queue). `hole_candidates` returns the outcomes that pass everything except the book (no recent
  book), at most `2 * hole_max_exchanges`, tightest spread first then exchange id: the runner
  reads their books.
* `ref` = the contract's fair value if a usable `fv` exists, else the contract mid.
  `price = floor_tick(ref * (1 - hole_depth_fraction))`, required `<= contract best bid -
  hole_min_ticks_below * 0.005` and `>= 0.05`.
* At most `hole_max_exchanges` (4) hole ideas, ranked by touch quantity x (1 / spread).
* `order_type="maker"`, `limit_price=price`, `expires_at = now + hole_ttl_s` (6 h), `max_units =
  hole_max_shares` (500), `levels = []`. `exit_plan = ExitPlan(kind="hole",
  target_bid=floor_tick(max(price + 0.05, ref - hole_exit_buffer)),
  time_stop_after_fill_s=hole_time_stop_s, exit_before_ts=<regime rule>, note="If it fills, sell
  when the bid recovers to 0.900, or after 12 h")`.
* `edge = hole_fill_prob * (target_bid - price)` (expected, tiny), `prob_win = hole_fill_prob`,
  `confidence = 0.5`, `score` = growth score of the fixed bet. Rationale explains the hole and that
  most such orders never fill, and that a resting order 40+ ticks under the bid usually has an
  unknown queue (it fills only when prices trade through it, §6.4); risks: adverse selection (it
  fills exactly when real news hits), the house can sweep its quotes, cancel before scheduled news
  (the order is cancelled automatically when a surge opens on the outcome, since the idea then
  disappears).

### 5.5 Changes to fade, carry and arbitrage (the wrong assumptions, fixed)

Numbers refer to `paper/codebase_map.md` §5.

1. **Settlement dates** (#9): `settles_before` replaces `_settles_before` for decisions: a
   settlement date equal to the Cup end (every real Cup market) is the closeout, not a
   resolution: "settles before" only if at least `settle_margin_s` (1 h) earlier. Risk text:
   "a settlement date is not a payout date: autosettlement can retry, REFUND and tournament
   rulings exist".
2. **Post-Cup valuation** (#1): remove the `score x 0.5` rule. Carry edges use `regime_ev(q =
   p_true, entry, contract_mid, ...)` (§5.2), so under `unknown`/`vwap_closeout` a carry that
   settles at the Cup end carries its uncalled-branch loss: a 0.97 favourite with q 0.98, mid
   0.965 and called 0.97 has vwap ev 0.0097, of which the 3% uncalled branch contributes -0.0101
   (payoff -0.3365 per share). Rationale states the regime, the called probability and the uncalled
   payoff. Carry gets `exit_plan = ExitPlan(kind="settle", hold_to_resolution=True,
   exit_before_ts=<regime rule>, note="Hold to settlement (pays 1.00 if it wins)")`.
3. **Carry fair value** (#11): `p_true = fv contract value` when a usable fair value exists (and
   say so, with `fv_uncertainty`), else the old rule of thumb, labelled "rule of thumb, not a
   measured edge" (`fv_uncertainty` = `DEFAULT_SIGMA["carry"]` 0.02).
4. **Fade** (#10, D36): `target_bid = target - half_spread`, `stop_bid = stop - half_spread`
   (contract terms). With a usable fair value: the target becomes the **nearer** of the
   half-reversion target and `fv_contract - half_spread` (never aim beyond fair value); if the fair
   value lies beyond the peak in the move's direction (the move went toward fair value) drop the
   fade. `gain = target_bid - entry`, `loss = entry - stop_bid`; drop the fade when `gain <
   params.min_fade_gain` (0.02). `p` = the attribution's reversion odds, capped by the **measured**
   net-of-spread reversion rate of participant-driven surges (`inputs.backtest.participant_reversion_rate`)
   when `n_participant >= 10`; the old `p = max(p, 0.65)` floor is gone. `fv_uncertainty` = 0.03.
   `exit_plan = ExitPlan(kind="bracket", target_bid, stop_bid, time_stop_ts = now + fade_horizon_h *
   3600)`.
5. **Arbitrage held past the Cup** (#2) and **sizing** (#4): arbitrage sets get a basket-style
   `exit_plan` and the same riskless-or-bounded `bet` rule as baskets (§5.3); sizes come from the
   policies, and `all_collateral` (when configured) is honoured for riskless sets.
6. **NO basket** (#3): the new `basket` kind (§5.3); arbitrage keeps the engine-violation path.
7. **Backtest reversion rate** (#12, D36): `backtest_fade` measures the fade return **net of the
   spread**: enter at the detection-time ask (YES) / `1 - bid` (NO) and exit at the horizon's
   bid, from tick bid/ask; candle-only points use an assumed spread of 0.02 (and a note says
   so). Add `net_of_spread: bool = True` (False restores the old mark-to-mark numbers) and keep
   the result fields; also fill `BacktestResult.n_participant` and `participant_reversion_rate`
   (the same measure over surges whose attribution verdict is "participants" only).
8. **Principles text** (#6, #7): replace "variance is your friend: take more, bigger,
   independent bets" with the chaser rule ("when behind, concentrate on your best edges, but a
   same-party slate is one bet on the national swing: size it as one"), and replace the
   diversification line with "races share one national polling error: a same-party slate of
   toss-ups is one bet; diversify only to protect a lead". Replace the settlement-date principle
   with the regime-aware one ("the Cup's end rule is unknown: prefer positions that pay under both a
   real resolution and a 5-hour VWAP closeout").
9. **Risk mode** (#5) stays as a label and score multiplier for backward compatibility, but the
   report now also carries the policy explanation (§5.7) and sizes come from the policy.

### 5.6 Sizing policies (`sizing.py`)

#### 5.6.1 Common mechanics

`kelly_units(p, gain, loss, W, k) = k * W * (p / loss - (1 - p) / gain)` (0 if <= 0). For a
binary bought at `c` this equals the legacy `size_position`; for a bracket it equals
`_bracket_shares` (proof: `f = (p - c)/(1 - c)` with `c = loss/(gain + loss)`). Keep the legacy
`+1e-9` before every `floor`.

**Kelly on the expected fill, not the touch** (D8). `bet_at_cost(bet, c)` re-prices a bet at cost `c`
(`p` fixed). `depth_kelly_units(bet, levels, W, k)` = the largest whole `u` with `u <=
kelly_units(bet_at_cost(bet, depth.avg_cost(levels, u)), W, k)` and `u <=` the units in `levels`
(binary search: Kelly units fall as the average cost rises). With empty `levels` (depth unknown)
it is Kelly at `bet.cost` (the touch): the legacy rule, and the chaser then caps its multiplier at
`UNKNOWN_DEPTH_MAX_KELLY` (0.5) because the fill may walk up to the limit.

**Certainty** (D9): `k_eff = k * certainty(edge, sigma)` with `certainty = edge^2 / (edge^2 +
sigma^2)` and `sigma = opp.fv_uncertainty` (else `DEFAULT_SIGMA[kind]`). Applied to `value` ideas
in both policies and to every directional idea in the chaser; the conservative policy leaves
fade/carry/arbitrage unscaled (legacy sizes).

For one idea (`size`), with `W = ctx.equity`:

| bet kind | uncapped units | caps |
|---|---|---|
| binary / bracket | `depth_kelly_units(bet, opp.levels, W, k_eff)` | `idea_cap * W / cost`; race cap; swing cap; total cap; cash; depth |
| riskless | `riskless_cap * W / cost` | total cap; cash (`cost` per unit, or `max(0, cost - floor)` with `all_collateral`, then notional `<= ALL_COLLATERAL_NOTIONAL_PCT * W / cost`); depth |
| bounded | `min(kelly_units(bet, W, k), cap * W / cost)` with `cap` = `riskless_cap` (conservative) / `CHASER_BASKET_CAP_PCT` (chaser) | total cap; cash; depth (no all-collateral credit) |
| fixed | `min(max_units or inf, cap_pct * W / cost)` | race cap; total cap; cash |

`cost` in the caps is the expected average fill when `levels` is known, else the touch.

* Race cap: `(exposure.by_race[race] + new cost) <= race_cap * W`.
* **Swing (tilt) cap** (D10): `national_tilt_d` is now delta-weighted: SUSQies gained for a 1-point
  national swing toward Democrats, `sum(qty x factor_delta)` over open positions and pending entries,
  set legs excluded. A new directional position may not push `|tilt_after| *
  params.national_swing_sd_pts` above `tilt_cap * W` (conservative: a one-standard-deviation,
  3-point national polling miss may cost at most 10% of equity), unless it reduces `|tilt|`. A
  toss-up YES share has delta `phi(0) / 7 = 0.057` per point, so the cap allows about 58,500
  toss-up shares on 100,000 (the old 30%-of-cost cap allowed 60,000, i.e. the same at toss-ups,
  but it also charged a 0.95 safe seat 0.95 per share although its delta is only 0.015).
* Total: `exposure.gross_cost + new cost <= total_cap * W`. Cash: `units * limit_price` (the
  engine reserves at the limit) `<= ctx.free_cash`. Depth: `units <= opp.max_units` when known.
  Race and swing caps do not apply to set kinds.
* `units = floor(min(...) + 1e-9)`; < 1 -> 0 with reason. `stake = units * cash_per_unit`;
  `avg_cost`, `certainty`, `factor_scale` on the decision.
* `capped_by` lists the binding cap(s) by name; `lines` explains every number, e.g.
  "Quarter-Kelly on YES with a fair value of 0.58 (edge +0.053, uncertainty 0.01: 97% certainty):
  5,144 shares", "Sized on the expected average fill of 0.526 across the book, not the 0.52
  touch", "Capped at 8% of equity per idea", "A 3-point national swing toward the Republicans would
  cost this portfolio 9,700 SUSQies: capped at 10% of equity", "Capped by the free cash (1,200
  SUSQies left after pending orders)", "The book offers 1,850 shares up to 0.545".
* `size_all(opps, ctx)`: sort by `score` desc then `idea_id`; size each in turn against
  exposure (cost, race, tilt, directional slots) and free cash updated by the earlier decisions;
  return `{idea_id: SizeDecision}`. `size(opp, ctx)` sizes one idea against the given exposure only
  (what the Strategy view shows: every card is sized as if it were the only new idea).

#### 5.6.2 `ConservativePolicy` (`name="conservative"`)

`kelly_mult 0.25`, `idea_cap 0.08`, `riskless_cap 0.08` (riskless and bounded sets), `total_cap
0.60`, `race_cap 0.12`, `tilt_cap 0.10` (swing cap). With an empty exposure, `equity = cash` and
**no stored book** it reproduces the legacy sizes of `fade_opportunity`, `carry_opportunity` and
`arbitrage_opportunities` exactly (existing tests); with a stored book the depth-walked rule may
give fewer shares than the legacy "Kelly at the touch, then cap by depth" rule: B updates exactly
those assertions and lists them. `explain(ctx)` = `{"policy": "conservative", "label", "mode":
None, "lines": ["Quarter-Kelly on the expected average fill, at most 8% of equity per idea, 12% per
race, 60% in total", "A 3-point national polling miss may cost at most 10% of equity: races share
one national polling error"], "bar", "M", "markov_ceiling", "ev_multiple"}` (bar fields filled when
`ctx.bar` is known, for information).

#### 5.6.3 Bar estimate and Markov ceiling

`estimate_bar(leaderboard, history, initial, days_left)` (D14):

* `value(entry) = entry["value"]` or `initial + entry["pnl"]`. `third`, `tenth`, `hundredth` =
  the 3rd/10th/100th highest values (None if missing). `floor = BAR_FLOOR_MULT (2) * initial`.
* **Level**: `low = max(third, floor)` (`method` "third", or "floor" when third is unknown or below
  the floor); **`value = low`**: sizing always uses the lower end of the range.
* **Growth, shown only, never compounded**: take the OLDEST history snapshot at least
  `BAR_MIN_HISTORY_S` (3 days) older than `leaderboard.at`; for each rank in `BAR_GROWTH_RANKS`
  (3..10) present in both, the linear daily growth `(v_now / v_then - 1) / days_between`;
  `growth_per_day` = their median, clipped to [0, `BAR_MAX_GROWTH_PER_DAY`] (2%/day); `high =
  min(third * (1 + growth * days_left), BAR_CAP_MULT * initial)`. With less than 3 days of history,
  `growth_per_day = None` and `high = low`. (Compounding 5%/day over 30 days multiplied the bar by
  4.3; one thin-book mark over 12 h could move M from 2.2 to 7.6.)
* `explanation` always includes: "Leaderboard values are mid-Cup marks (cash plus positions at
  current prices), not settled balances; thin-market marks can be inflated.", the range ("Top-3
  bar: 221,500 now; at the recent pace of +1.0% a day it could reach 287,950 by the Cup end.
  Sizing uses the lower end."), and how the value was formed.

`markov_ceiling(ev_multiple, M) = min(1, ev_multiple / M)` with `ev_multiple = 1 +
ctx.expected_profit / ctx.equity` (expected profit of open positions only). `markov_sentence(ev, M)`
(exact, D32): "A strategy whose expected multiple is 1.04x reaches 2.2x with probability at most
47%; with no edge (fair prices) the bound is 1/M = 45%." The bound is about the strategy whose
expected multiple is `ev`, not about every strategy.

#### 5.6.4 `ChaserPolicy` (`name="chaser"`)

`M = bar.value / ctx.equity` (bar.value is the low end; None without a bar). **Mode with
hysteresis** (D15): the raw mode follows the table; when `ctx.prev_mode` is set and differs, the
policy switches across the 1.3 / 10 thresholds only when `M` is beyond the threshold by
`MODE_BAND` (10%: leaving chase for out_of_reach needs M > 11, coming back needs M < 9; near <->
chase needs M < 1.17 / M > 1.43) **and** `now - ctx.mode_since >= MODE_MIN_HOLD_S` (6 h);
`swing` (time-driven) and `unknown_bar` switch at once. The engine stores `prev_mode` and
`mode_since` per portfolio.

| mode | when | rules |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown_bar` | no bar | half-Kelly, idea 15%, race 25%, total 80%, swing cap 30% |
| `near` | `M <= 1.3` | half-Kelly, idea 12%, race 25%, total 70%, swing cap 30% |
| `chase` | `1.3 < M <= 10` and `days_left > SWING_DAYS (2)` | full Kelly, idea 25%, race 35%, total 95%, swing cap 40%, **concentrate**: `top_k = 5` directional slots |
| `swing` | `1.3 < M <= 10` and `days_left <= 2` | as `chase` without the swing cap, plus **one bold-to-goal bet** |
| `out_of_reach` | `M > 10` | exactly the conservative rules; line "The bar is more than 10x your value: the prize is out of reach in expectation, so size for a steady record (Smart Score) instead" |

Rules that apply in every chaser mode except `out_of_reach`:

* **Certainty shrink** on every directional idea (D9), and `k <= UNKNOWN_DEPTH_MAX_KELLY` (0.5)
  when `opp.levels` is empty (D8).
* **A correlated slate is one bet** (D11): for a directional idea with `factor_delta` sign `s != 0`,
  `n_same` = the held/pending directional ideas with the same sign (`exposure.directional_sign`)
  plus the new ideas selected in this `size_all` with that sign; its Kelly units are multiplied by
  `factor_scale(n_same) = 1 / (1 + (n_same - 1) * FACTOR_RHO)` (rho 0.5: 5 Democratic-leaning
  toss-ups get 1/3 of their own Kelly each, about 18% of equity in all instead of 55%). Then the
  mode's swing cap (`|tilt_after| * national_swing_sd_pts <= swing_cap * W`) bounds the slate.
  Line: "4 Democratic-leaning ideas share one national polling error: each gets 40% of its own
  Kelly size, so together they are about one Kelly bet on the swing".
* **`top_k` is real** (D12): `slots = top_k - len(exposure.directional)` (held and pending
  directional positions count). The best `slots` new directional ideas by score get capital; the
  others get 0 with `capped_by=["top_k"]`. When `slots == 0`, the best new idea whose score exceeds
  the weakest held one's by `REPLACE_MARGIN` (50%) gets `units = 0`, `replaces = <that idea_id>`
  and the line "Better than the weakest held idea (score 0.80 vs 0.40): sell that one first"; the
  engine exits the held position (reason "replaced") and the new idea can enter once a slot is
  free. At most one replacement per step.
* **One bold bet** (swing mode only, D13): only when `exposure.bold_idea` is None. Among the
  selected directional ideas with `settles_before_cup_end` True or `called_prob >= 0.85`, the
  best-scored one whose cost `c` (expected average fill, else the touch) satisfies `c <=
  bold_max_cost(bar, W) = cap * W / (bar - W + cap * W)` (W 100,000, bar 221,500, cap 60%: c <=
  0.3306) and whose depth (when known) holds the units gets `stake = min((bar - W) * c / (1 - c),
  SWING_BOLD_CAP_PCT * W, free cash)`, `units = floor(stake / c)`, `bold=True`, line "Bold bet: if it
  wins, your value reaches the 221,500 bar; it loses 52,071 otherwise" (c 0.30). If no idea qualifies,
  every idea is sized as `chase` and the line says why ("No bold bet: the cheapest qualifying
  contract costs 0.45; above 0.33 even a 60% stake cannot reach the bar (it would win to 173,333),
  so a swing would waste the variance"). Never two bold bets: their win states would not be joint.
* Set kinds (riskless or bounded): `CHASER_BASKET_CAP_PCT` (20%) per set (D27); fixed kinds: the
  conservative rule. Hard caps and "never more than the free cash" apply in every mode.

`explain(ctx)` lines must cover: the bar, its range and how it was estimated, `M`,
`markov_sentence`, the mode (and "kept by hysteresis" when applicable) and its rules, "Correlated,
same-party ideas are sized together as one bet on the national swing: concentrated, not
multiplied", and "Sizes never exceed the free cash".

`get_policy("conservative" | "chaser")` returns a fresh object; anything else raises ValueError.

## 6. Package C: the paper engine (`paper.py`), the backtest (`backtest.py`) and the event study (`study.py`)

### 6.1 Portfolios, the pre-registered headline, and capital

`PaperConfig.portfolios` defaults to `default_portfolios(config.sizing)` (E passes the user's
`--sizing`), in this order:

1. **`human:<sizing>`: the pre-registered headline** (D4, D7). The user's chosen sizing policy on
   every trade kind, at **human speed**: `min_latency_s = HUMAN_LATENCY_S` (240 s) and
   `max_fill_delay_s = HUMAN_MAX_FILL_DELAY_S` (360 s), i.e. it fills from the first book observed
   at least 4 minutes after the decision (a person reads the dashboard and types the order). This is
   the question the user asked: would *I* make money copying these ideas by hand?
2. `policy:conservative` and `policy:chaser` at bot speed (2 s latency, books read at the next 30-s
   step): what an automated executor could reach; an upper bound for manual copying.
3. One conservative bot-speed portfolio per kind (`kind:value`, `kind:basket`, `kind:hole`,
   `kind:fade`, `kind:carry`, `kind:arbitrage`), equal capital, so the per-kind rows compare edges.

`paper.headline_id(config)` = `config.headline_portfolio` or `f"human:{config.sizing}"` (falling back
to the first portfolio). The headline is the only portfolio whose verdict can reach every level;
every other portfolio is **exploratory** (§6.9). Per-portfolio latency: an order uses its portfolio's
`min_latency_s` / `max_fill_delay_s` when set, else the config's.

Every portfolio starts with the same capital. Start capital, in order: `PaperConfig.start_capital`
/ the reset request's value ("set by you"); the real account value ("account value"); the real cash
("cash"); the tournament's initial balance ("initial balance"); 100,000 ("default 100,000"). The
runner passes the value and its source to `engine.start`. Portfolios are independent what-ifs: they
share signals and reads but not liquidity, cash or positions.

### 6.2 One step (`run_step` and `PaperEngine.step`)

`run_step(engine, inputs, obs, fulfil, signal_fn, candidates_fn, decided_at)`:

1. `inputs.resting_exchanges = engine.resting_exchanges()`;
   `plan = engine.wanted_reads(obs.now, obs, candidates_fn(inputs))`.
2. `f = fulfil(plan, obs.now)` (a `paper.Fulfilment`); merge (`obs.books[eid]` replaced by a newer
   observation and attached to `inputs.latest[eid].book` in the stored-book shape; `obs.trades[eid]`
   extended, sorted by ts, deduplicated by trade id).
3. `signals = signal_fn(inputs, inputs.params)`.
4. `report = engine.step(now, obs, signals, inputs, tape_truncated=...)` with `now = decided_at()`
   (live: the clock after the reads) else `obs.now`; the read counts and `f.skipped` go into the
   report.

`PaperEngine.step(now, obs, signals, inputs)` runs these phases **for each portfolio, in config
order**, then persists:

0. **Coverage** (§6.11): `CoverageClock.tick(now)`. After a gap longer than `gap_cancel_s` (300 s)
   every resting maker order becomes `cancelling` with `cancel_at` = the last step time before the
   gap (the bot could not have managed it while down; prints from the downtime never fill it), and
   `StepReport.gap_s` is set.
1. **Settlements and closures first** (§6.6, D23): settle positions, cancel orders on settled or
   closed outcomes. Nothing fills on an outcome that settled or left `obs.open_ids`.
2. **Taker fills** for orders with status `pending` (§6.3). Orders whose `created_at +
   max_fill_delay_s < now` (their portfolio's value) and are still unfilled expire ("No order book
   read within 120 s of the decision: not filled").
3. **Maker fills** for `resting` and `cancelling` orders from `obs.trades` (§6.4). A resting
   order whose idea was absent from `signals` for `maker_cancel_after_missing_steps` (2)
   consecutive steps, or whose `expires_at` passed, becomes `cancelling` with `cancel_at` = that
   step's time (or `expires_at`): the real order would have rested until then, so prints up to
   `cancel_at` still fill it. It closes (`cancelled` / `expired`, "The conditions for this
   resting order no longer hold" / "Expired unfilled") at the first step whose tape for that
   exchange was read after `cancel_at`, or `max_fill_delay_s` after `cancel_at` without such a
   read (no further fills).
4. **Marks** of every open position (§6.7).
5. **Exits** (§6.5): create exit orders (taker, `purpose="exit"`), filled from a later step on;
   forced legging-out exits; replacements requested by the chaser.
6. **Entries**: eligible signals = kind in `TRADE_KINDS` and in the portfolio's `kinds`; quote
   not stale; outcome open; `now < cup_end`; no open position, pending/resting order or
   cooldown (`reentry_cooldown_s` after an idea closes) for the same `idea_id`; no open position
   or order on any exchange the idea touches (one net position per exchange); the portfolio
   below `max_open_positions`; **set ideas only when every leg has a real book observed at most
   `set_book_max_age_s` (60 s) before `now`** (else the idea is *deferred*: counted in
   `StepReport.sets_deferred` and its legs are requested as one read group next step, §6.10, D30).
   Size them with `policy.size_all(eligible, ctx)` (§6.8 builds `ctx`); create at most
   `max_new_orders_per_step` orders (best score first): taker ideas -> `pending` buy order(s) at
   `limit_price` (sets: one order per leg, shared `group_id`, each at the leg's `limit`, qty = units;
   set units are additionally capped by `depth.walk_set` of the fresh leg books up to the legs'
   limits); maker ideas -> `resting` buy order at `limit_price`, `expires_at`, `queue_ahead =
   queue_ahead_at(...)` (§6.4). Every order records `decision_touch` (the contract ask for buys from
   the decision quote) and `latency_s` (its portfolio's). Reserve cash (§6.8). `reason` = the
   idea's first rationale line. A decision with `replaces` starts that exit (step 5 of the next
   step uses reason "replaced"; the new idea is not entered now).
7. **Event study** (§6.14): `study.observe(now, obs, signals, traded_ids)` once per step (not per
   portfolio); newly completed events are persisted.
8. **Equity points** for every portfolio (§6.7), drawdown update; a point is stored at most every
   `equity_min_spacing_s` (60 s) per portfolio (the in-memory curve keeps the same points).
9. **Persistence** (§6.11); the final snapshot when covered hours first reach `target_hours` (the
   run continues afterwards in the dashboard; `paper --hours N` ends it, §7.5).

Orders created at a step are never filled in that same step (they need an observation made
after `created_at + latency`, and every observation in this step predates the decision).

### 6.3 Taker fill model (no look-ahead, real depth, no liquidity re-use)

For a pending order on exchange `e` created at `t0` with latency `lat` (its portfolio's):

* The fill observation is **the first** `BookObservation` for `e` with `t0 + lat <= observed_at <=
  t0 + max_fill_delay_s` (the engine remembers, per order, which observation it used; a later,
  better book is never chosen instead). Live: the book read at the first step whose read plan
  requested it after `t0 + lat` (bot speed: the next step; human speed: the step at or after 4
  minutes). Backtest: the adapter supplies it (§6.12).
* No fill while the outcome is not in `obs.open_ids` or its bulk quote is older than `stale_quote_s`
  (the order waits, then expires).
* Levels: `contract_levels(book, side, action)`, minus liquidity this portfolio already took on
  that exchange side within `consumed_memory_s` (**1800 s**, never shorter than
  `reentry_cooldown_s`, D35): for every earlier fill level, subtract its quantity from the newer
  book's level at the same YES price, or, if there is none, at the nearest level within
  `consumed_match_ticks` (1 tick) (never below 0), whatever the newer book shows (we never traded,
  so a real refill or a one-tick re-quote of the same house rung cannot be ours to take twice).
  Older consumption is forgotten.
* `depth.walk(levels, remaining, limit, buy)`: best first, only prices no worse than the limit,
  whole shares. One `PaperFill` per order per step (`levels` = what was walked, `price` = average,
  `book_at`, `latency_s`, `slippage = price - decision_touch` for buys, `synthetic` when the book is
  synthetic). **Entry** orders are immediate-or-cancel: the unfilled remainder is cancelled,
  `close_reason="The book had only 1,200 of 2,000 shares at 0.545 or better"`, and the order is
  added to the portfolio's *unfilled* list (§6.13).
* Sets: legs fill independently against their own books (the read plan reads a group's books in
  the same step, or none). After every leg has an outcome (filled, partly filled, cancelled or
  expired), `sets = min(filled qty over legs)`; any leg's excess over `sets` is **legging
  residue**: an exit order for it is created at once (purpose "exit", reason "Legging: the other
  legs filled only 300 sets", limit = 0.005, i.e. sell into whatever the next book shows), closed as
  a trade with `exit_reason="legging"`. Deferring set entries until every leg has a fresh book, and
  capping units by the thinner leg (§6.2), keeps this residue to what moved in one step.
* **Exit** (sell) orders walk the bids of the contract (`contract_levels(..., "sell")`) down to
  their limit; shares the book cannot absorb stay pending and fill from the next observation made
  after the one already used (never the same observation twice), until `max_fill_delay_s` after
  their own `created_at`; then they expire and the next exit evaluation creates a new one.

### 6.4 Maker fill model (trade-through, known queue)

A resting buy of contract `side` at contract price `L` rests at YES price `P = L` (YES: a YES bid)
or `P = 1 - L` (NO: a YES ask). At placement (D19):

* `queue_ahead = queue_ahead_at(book, side, L, now, config)`: the displayed quantity at exactly
  `P` on our side of the newest real book (YES bids for a YES order, YES asks for a NO order); 0 when
  `P` lies strictly inside the displayed range without a level there; **None** (unknown) when there
  is no book, the book is older than `queue_book_max_age_s` (120 s), or `P` lies beyond the last
  displayed level on that side (a hole order 46 ticks under the bid usually does: the queue there is
  unknown, not empty).

For each tape print `(id, ts, price q, size z, taker side)` in `obs.trades[e]`, oldest first, with
`ts > created_at + latency`, `ts <= now`, `ts <= cancel_at` (when cancelling), not in
`seen_trade_ids`:

* **Through**: YES order: `q < P - 1e-9`; NO order: `q > P + 1e-9`. Fill `min(remaining, z)` at `L`.
* **At the price** (`|q - P| <= 1e-9`): only when `queue_ahead` is known **and** the print's taker hit
  our side (taker "NO", i.e. a YES seller, for a resting YES bid; taker "YES" for a resting NO order,
  i.e. a YES ask); a print with an unknown taker side does not count. The print first reduces
  `queue_ahead` (the reduction carries over to later prints and steps); of what is left,
  `queue_haircut x left` (0.5) fills, at most `remaining`.
* Never better than `L`, never more than the printed size, never from prints before placement.
* `last_trade_ts` / `seen_trade_ids` (newest 500) advance; one `PaperFill` per order per step
  (`liquidity="maker"`, `trade_ids`, `book_at` = the newest print used).
* A tape read that failed or was skipped produces no fills (the order keeps waiting). A tape read
  that came back **truncated** (§6.10: still full after `max_tape_pages`) sets `order.tape_gap` and
  counts in `StepReport.tape_gaps`: prints between the cursor and the oldest print returned may have
  been missed (conservative, now visible, D40).

### 6.5 Exits

For each open position, in this order, the first rule that fires creates an exit order for the
whole quantity (baskets: one order per leg, shared `group_id`). Bids are the contract's touch
bid from the bulk quote (YES bid, or `1 - YES ask` for NO); a stale quote fires nothing except
the regime and time rules. Every exit order records `planned_price` (the price the rule aimed
for) so exit slippage is reported (§6.13).

1. Settlement (phase 1, not an order).
2. `exit_before_ts` reached -> reason "regime", limit 0.005.
3. `stop_bid` and bid <= stop -> "stop", limit 0.005.
4. Target: `target_bid` and bid >= target -> "target", limit = `target_bid - 0.005`, planned
   `target_bid`; value positions with `dynamic_fv_target` and a usable current fair value use
   `target = floor_tick(fv_contract - half_spread - exit_buffer)` instead (reason "fair_value" when
   the fair value moved, "converged" otherwise). **Baskets** (D29): when the touch per-set proceeds
   `sum(contract bids of the legs) - set cost >= min_set_profit`, evaluate the **depth-walked**
   proceeds of selling the whole set quantity: `depth.liquidation_proceeds` of each leg's qty into
   that leg's sell levels from its newest real book at most `mark_book_max_age_s` old; exit
   ("converged") only when `depth-walked proceeds - set cost x sets >= min_set_profit x sets`, each
   leg's limit = the lowest bid level that walk used. A leg without such a book: request the group's
   books (priority 1) and decide at the next step.
5. `time_stop_ts` reached, or `time_stop_after_fill_s` after `first_fill_at` -> "time", limit 0.005.
6. A chaser `replaces` decision names this position -> "replaced", limit = touch bid - 0.005.
7. `hold_to_resolution` or none fired: keep.

**Legging out** (D29): when some legs of a basket exit group have filled (wholly or partly) and
another leg is still unfilled `max_fill_delay_s` after the group's creation (or its order expired),
the remainder of every leg is force-exited at limit 0.005 with reason "legging" (a set with one leg
sold is a naked position, not a basket). A position with a pending exit order gets no second one.
The trade's `exit_reason` is the reason of the exit order that closed it (a basket closed partly by
"converged" and partly by "legging" records "legging").

### 6.6 Settlement, closure without a ruling, Cup end

* **When a settlement is known** (D23): live, when the tracker has read it (`detected_at`; it reads
  settled markets every 600 s and when outcomes go missing); replay, at `max(settled_on,
  detected_at or settled_on)` (§6.12). Phase 1 runs before fills, so a pending order is cancelled
  before it could fill on a book read after the outcome settled.
* An outcome in `obs.settlements` with `payout_yes` 1/0: every open position on it is paid
  `qty x payout` (YES) or `qty x (1 - payout)` (NO) as a `PaperFill(action="settle",
  liquidity="settlement", purpose="settlement", price=payout per share)`; open orders on it are
  cancelled; the position closes (trade `exit_reason="settled"`). `refund=True`: proceeds = the
  position's cost (`exit_reason="refund"`). `payout_yes is None` and not refund: treat as below.
* An outcome not in `obs.open_ids`, not settled: positions become `frozen` with flag
  `closed_no_ruling`, no orders, no exits; they unfreeze if the outcome reappears. **Frozen
  positions are "unvalued"** (D45): they count 0 in `equity_liq` and `pnl_liq`;
  their last liquidation value is shown separately (`PortfolioSummary.unvalued`) and the verdict
  sentence names how many there are. Such closures often follow news, so the last mark could hide a
  loss.
* At and after `cup_end`: no entries and no exits; positions keep their last liquidation value,
  flag `post_cup`, until settled.

### 6.7 Valuation

Per open position, every step (D31):

* `liq_value, depth_state = liquidation_value(side, qty, book, quote, last_real_book=, now=, config=)`:
  * **fresh**: the newest REAL observation for the exchange (never `source == "synthetic"`) at most
    `mark_book_max_age_s` (900 s) old **and** consistent with the quote (its best level within one
    tick of the touch); walked with `depth.liquidation_proceeds`; shares beyond its depth are worth 0;
  * **stale**: else the last real book at most `mark_depth_max_age_s` (1 h) old, its sell levels
    shifted so the best equals today's touch bid (`depth.shift_levels`), walked the same way;
  * **unknown**: else `min(qty, depth_unknown_full_shares) x touch + max(0, qty - 100) x touch x (1 -
    depth_unknown_haircut)` (100 shares at the touch, the rest at half of it: a 21,000-share position
    on an unseen book is never valued as if it all sold at the touch);
  * no bid at all: 0 ("unknown").
* `mark_value` = `mid_value(side, qty, quote)` (reference). `fv_value` = `qty x fv_contract` when a
  usable fair value exists: **the model's own opinion** of the position (it is valued by the same
  outside price that generated it, so `fv_value - cost` is about `edge x qty` by construction); it
  is never a verdict input and never shown without `FV_MODEL_LABEL` (D41).
* Portfolio: `positions_liq = sum(liq_value of open, non-frozen positions)`, `equity_liq = cash +
  positions_liq - outstanding collateral advances`; likewise `equity_mark`, `equity_fv` (positions
  without a fair value count at liquidation value; None when no position has one). `pnl_liq =
  equity_liq - start_capital`; `unrealized_pnl_liq = positions_liq - cost of open positions`;
  `unvalued` = last liquidation value of frozen positions; `depth_unknown_share` /
  `depth_stale_share` = the share of `equity_liq` in positions marked "unknown" / "stale".
* The read plan re-reads mark books largest liquidation value first (§6.10).
* Drawdown: running peak of `equity_liq`; `max_drawdown_abs = max(peak - equity_liq)`,
  `max_drawdown = max((peak - equity_liq) / peak)`.

### 6.8 Accounting

* `cash` includes `reserved_cash`; `free_cash = cash - reserved_cash`.
* Entry buy orders reserve `qty x limit_price` at creation (sets: per leg); never create an order
  whose reservation exceeds `free_cash` (the policy already sized within it). On a fill: `cash
  -= qty x price`, the reservation shrinks by `qty x limit_price`; on close the rest is released.
* `all_collateral` (riskless sets only): the entry reservation is `units x max(0, limit_price -
  floor)` split evenly across the legs (the policy sized on that cash need); when the legs of a
  set fill, the full cost is debited and `advance = min(set cost, floor) x sets` is credited back
  at once and recorded on the legs' `collateral_advance` (split evenly); it is repaid (subtracted
  from proceeds) when the set is sold or settles. Equity subtracts outstanding advances.
* Sells add `qty x price` to cash; settlement adds the payout. A partial exit moves
  `qty x (price - avg_cost)` into the position's `realized_pnl`; `avg_cost` is unchanged.
* A position closes when its qty reaches 0; one `PaperTrade` per closed position, or per basket
  when its last leg closes (`legs` lists each leg; `qty` = sets; `cost`, `proceeds`, `pnl`,
  `return_pct = pnl / cost`, `hold_hours`, `profit_per_capital_day = pnl / (cost x max(hold days,
  1/24))`, `entered_at`, `direction` = sign of the position's swing exposure at entry ("D"/"R"/"N";
  sets "N"), `synthetic`). The idea enters cooldown.
* `SizingContext` for a portfolio: `equity = equity_liq`, `cash`, `free_cash`, `start_capital`,
  `cup_end`, `days_left = max(0, (cup_end - now) / 86400)`, `exposure` (gross = cost of open
  positions + reserved cash; by race (race key, else `market:<id>`); by idea; `national_tilt_d =
  sum(qty x factor_delta)` over open positions and pending entries, set legs excluded; open count;
  `directional` / `directional_sign` over open and pending binary/bracket ideas; `bold_idea`),
  `bar = inputs.bar`, `expected_profit = sum(qty x edge_per_unit)` with **`edge_per_unit` of a set
  leg = the set's edge / n_legs** (so a basket's edge is counted once, D32), `my_rank =
  inputs.my_rank`, `regime = config.regime`, `all_collateral`, `races = obs.races`, `prev_mode` /
  `mode_since` (the portfolio's chaser mode memory), `params = config.params`.
* Ids (deterministic, per run): `run_id = "run-<int(started_at)>"` (suffix `-2`, `-3` on
  collision); `order_id = "<pid>:o<n>"`, `fill_id = "<pid>:f<n>"`, `trade_id = "<pid>:t<n>"`,
  `basket_id = "<pid>:b<n>"`, `position_id = "<pid>:<eid>"`; event ids `"<idea_id>@<int(t0)>"`.

### 6.9 Verdict (`compute_verdict`)

Revision 1's verdict was anti-conservative (D1-D5): a percentile cluster bootstrap over 8 races
under-covers; clustering by race cannot see the day's national shock; closed trades exclude the
open losers of a strategy whose winners exit at a target; and the best of 8 portfolios gets
highlighted. Revision 2:

**What is counted** (D1). `engine.ideas(pid)` returns one `IdeaOutcome` per idea the portfolio
**entered** (any fill): closed ideas at realised P&L, open ideas at realised + current liquidation
P&L (`liq_value - cost` of the open quantity + partial realised P&L); `entered_at` = the entry
order's decision time; `direction` = "D"/"R"/"N"; `race_key` (else `market:<id>`); `cost`;
`synthetic` (any synthetic fill, replays); `frozen`. `compute_verdict` drops synthetic and frozen
ideas (counting them in `synthetic_excluded` / `unvalued_positions`).

**Statistic** (D2). Two-way cluster-robust t-interval of the mean P&L per idea,
`cluster_t_interval(pnls, race_keys, time_blocks, level=L)` with time block = `(int(entered_at //
TIME_BLOCK_S), direction)` (2-hour blocks x party direction), `G = min(#race clusters, #time
clusters)`, `G - 1` degrees of freedom (`t_quantile`, stdlib). The same interval of the per-idea
return on capital `pnl / cost` (so a 50-share hole does not count like an 8,000-set basket).
`L = CI_LEVEL` (0.90) for the headline; `L = 1 - FAMILY_ALPHA / n_portfolios` (0.10 / 9 -> 98.9%)
for exploratory portfolios. Win rate (Wilson, z 1.645) over **closed** trades only, labelled
"closed trades only: biased toward quick winners".

**Thresholds**: `MIN_COVERED_HOURS` 12, `MIN_IDEAS` 30, `MIN_CLUSTERS` 20 (on G). Below any: level
`insufficient`.

**Levels** (D3), in order:

1. `insufficient`: a threshold unmet.
2. `negative` if `ci_high < 0` and `pnl_liq < 0` (the guards below do not soften a loss).
3. A **favourable** result (`ci_low > 0` **and** `return_ci_low > 0` and `pnl_liq > 0`) first meets
   the guards (D5); any guard that fires makes it `inconclusive`, with its reason: `pnl_ex_best <= 0`
   (P&L without the single best idea, open or closed); `depth_unknown_share >
   MAX_DEPTH_UNKNOWN_SHARE` (0.20); `swing_share = |national_tilt_d| x national_swing_sd_pts /
   equity_liq > MAX_SWING_SHARE` (0.10: the result could be one national swing); `top_race_share =
   max over races of |race P&L| / |pnl_liq| > MAX_TOP_RACE_SHARE` (0.50). If no guard fires:
   * `positive` only for the headline, and only when `covered_hours >= POSITIVE_MIN_HOURS` (48) on at
     least `POSITIVE_MIN_DAYS` (2) UTC days with `>= DAY_MIN_HOURS` (6) covered each, and the ideas
     entered on each of the two most recent such days sum to a positive P&L;
   * otherwise `promising` ("promising, not proof"): the strongest level any one-day run can reach,
     and the highest level an exploratory portfolio can ever reach (`EXPLORATORY_MAX_LEVEL`).
4. Else `inconclusive`.

Why these numbers (simulation `arch2/verdict_v2_sim.py`, zero true edge, nominal one-sided 5%):
with balanced party directions the two-way t-interval's false-positive rate is 4.0-5.5% (revision
1's bootstrap: 5.3-6.3%); when 80% of ideas lean one party, one day's national shock makes any
within-day interval anti-conservative (11.1% at a shock of 0.25 x the per-idea noise, 14.3% at
0.5x): that is why one day can only be "promising" and the swing guard exists; requiring two days
each positive brings it back to 4.5-5.3% at shocks up to 0.25x (10.1% at an extreme 0.5x, which the
swing guard then catches). With 9 portfolios at a nominal 5% each, picking the best would be wrong
about a third of the time; hence one pre-registered headline.

**Sentences** (exact templates; `{pnl}` = `f"{x:+,.0f}"`, `{lo}`/`{hi}` = `f"{x:+,.1f}"`, `{h}` =
`f"{x:.1f}"`, `{L}` = `f"{100 * level:.0f}"` (98.9 -> "99"), plural `s` on idea/race/day/position):

* insufficient: `Not enough evidence yet: {n} idea{s} entered ({c} closed, {o} open) across {k} race{s} in {h} h observed. A verdict needs at least 30 ideas, 20 independent groups of races and 2-hour periods, and 12 hours; P&L so far {pnl} SUSQies at liquidation value.`
* promising: `Promising, not proof: {pnl} SUSQies at liquidation value over {h} h observed and {n} ideas ({c} closed, {o} open); the {L}% interval for the average idea is {lo} to {hi} SUSQies, and the result stays positive without the best idea ({ex}). One day cannot separate an edge from one national swing: confirm it on a second day before acting.`
* positive: `Profitable so far: {pnl} SUSQies at liquidation value over {h} h observed on {d} days and {n} ideas; the {L}% interval for the average idea is {lo} to {hi} SUSQies, positive on each of the last two days and without the best idea ({ex}).`
* negative: `Losing so far: {pnl} SUSQies at liquidation value over {h} h observed and {n} ideas ({c} closed, {o} open); the {L}% interval for the average idea is {lo} to {hi} SUSQies, entirely below zero.`
* inconclusive: `Inconclusive: {pnl} SUSQies at liquidation value over {h} h observed and {n} ideas ({c} closed, {o} open), but ` + (`the {L}% interval for the average idea ({lo} to {hi} SUSQies) includes zero.` when the interval includes zero, else the first guard's clause: `without the best idea the result is {ex}.` / `{pct} of the value is marked without a recent order book.` / `a 3-point national swing would move this portfolio by {pct} of its value, so the result may be one swing, not an edge.` / `one race supplies {pct} of the P&L.`)
* Suffixes, in this order, when they apply: ` {u} position{s} whose market closed without a ruling {is/are} left out (last value {v}).`; for exploratory portfolios the prefix `Exploratory (one of {N} portfolios, judged at the stricter {L}% level): ` before the sentence.

`reasons`: one line per unmet threshold ("only 7 ideas entered (needs 30)", "only 12 independent
groups (needs 20): 15 races, 12 time blocks"), per guard, and always: "Closed: {c} ideas, {pnl_c}
realised; open: {o} ideas, {pnl_o} at liquidation value" and the closed-only win rate with its
label. `caveats` = `CAVEATS` (+ `DEMO_CAVEAT` in demo). `thresholds = {"min_hours": 12,
"min_ideas": 30, "min_clusters": 20, "positive_hours": 48, "positive_days": 2}`. The verdict is
recomputed when the runner publishes (every step); it is O(n), no resampling.

### 6.10 Read plan and budget (the runner)

`engine.wanted_reads(now, obs, candidates)` returns requests in priority order, one per
`(kind, exchange)`; requests that must be read together share a `group` (D30):

1. Books for exchanges with pending taker orders (entries, exits, all legs of a group) whose fill
   window has opened (`now >= created_at + latency`) and that have no observation yet satisfying
   it; `after = created_at + latency`; group = the order group.
2. Books for deferred set entries (every leg, one group) and for basket exits waiting for books.
3. Tape for exchanges with resting orders, if not read in the last `trade_poll_s` (60 s), oldest
   poll first, and for exchanges with `cancelling` orders not read since their `cancel_at`
   (regardless of `trade_poll_s`, first); `since` = the oldest `last_trade_ts`/`created_at` of
   their orders.
4. Books for open positions whose newest real observation is older than `mark_refresh_s` (300 s),
   **largest liquidation value first** (D31).
5. Books for exchanges with resting orders whose newest book is older than
   `resting_book_refresh_s` (600 s) (keeps hole ideas and their queue estimate alive, D55).
6. Books for `candidates` (hole candidates) and for the top 3 ideas the previous step sized > 0
   whose `levels` were empty, if their newest book is older than `2 x 30 s`.

`PaperRunner` fulfils requests in order while `limiter.room() > 0`, at most
`max_book_reads_per_step` (8) books and `max_trade_reads_per_step` (2) tape **pages** per step; a
group is read only if all of its requests fit, else all are skipped. The rest are `skipped`
(counted in `StepReport.reads_skipped` and `budget.reads_skipped_last_step`). A reader returning
None is a failed read (no data, no retry this step). The runner never calls anything on the reader
except `book` and `trades`. A tape read pages with the cursor up to `min(max_tape_pages, pages
left in the step)`; a last page still full (200) marks the read `truncated` (D40).

**Budget arithmetic** (live, 30-s steps): at most 8 + 2 = 10 reads per step = 20/min, the paper
budget's own limit is 20/min (6/min while the tracker's backfill queue is not empty), and every
paper read also needs a free slot above the snapshot loop's reserve (§9).

### 6.11 Persistence, restart, coverage, fingerprints, reset

* After every step: `paper_save_run(run_id, started_at, config, state, now)`; changed orders via
  `paper_put_orders`; new fills, trades, equity points and completed events via the `paper_add_*` /
  `paper_put_events` methods. `config` = `{"fingerprint", "code_version", "paper": PaperConfig.to_dict()}`.
* `state()` JSON (version 2): `{"version": 2, "run_id", "started_at", "start_capital",
  "capital_source", "target_hours", "steps", "last_step_at", "last_step_seconds", "coverage":
  CoverageClock.to_dict(), "counters": {pid: {"o", "f", "t", "b"}}, "portfolios": {pid: {"cash",
  "reserved_cash", "peak_equity", "max_drawdown", "max_drawdown_abs", "realized_pnl", "fills",
  "positions": [PaperPosition], "orders": [open PaperOrder], "consumed": [[exchange_id, book_side,
  yes_price, qty, at]], "cooldowns": {idea_id: until}, "missing": {order_id: steps}, "fill_obs":
  {order_id: observed_at}, "mode": {"prev_mode", "mode_since"}, "unfilled": [up to
  unfilled_tracked {"exchange_id", "side", "qty", "decision_touch", "created_at", "kind"}],
  "deferred": [idea_id], "diagnostics": {...}}}, "last_real_books": {exchange_id: BookObservation}
  (exchanges with open positions only, at most 1 h old), "study": SignalStudy.state(), "final": null
  | {"at", "reason", "verdicts": {pid: Verdict}, "portfolios": [PortfolioSummary], "study"},
  "last_signals": {"count", "by_kind", "at", "top_bookless": [idea_id]}}`.
* **Covered hours** (D48): `CoverageClock` adds `min(now - last_step_at, 3 x interval_s)` per step
  (`interval_s` = 30 live, `step_s` in a backtest); longer gaps go into `gaps` ([{"from", "to"}],
  newest 50). `hours_run` everywhere = covered hours; `wall_hours` is shown next to it ("21.3 h
  observed of the 24-hour test (2 gaps)"). The final snapshot and every threshold use covered hours.
  A laptop that slept 10 h overnight resumes with 10 h less coverage, and its resting orders do not
  fill on prints from the downtime (phase 0).
* **Continuing a run** (D49): construction loads `paper_load_run(None)` (the newest run without
  `ended_at`) and continues it only when its stored fingerprint equals
  `config_fingerprint(current config, code_version)` (sha256 of the canonical JSON of
  `PaperConfig.to_dict()` without `target_hours` and `demo`, plus `pipeline.code_version()`: the
  package version and the git commit). A mismatch (a changed `--regime`, `--all-collateral`,
  `--paper-capital`, `--sizing`, any StrategyParams default, new code) ends the old run with reason
  "settings changed" (final snapshot written) and starts a fresh one. The Simulation view shows the
  run's settings.
* **Ending a run** (D60): `end_run(now, reason)` always writes the final snapshot (`{"at",
  "reason", "verdicts", "portfolios", "study"}`) before `paper_end_run`; open positions become
  trades with `exit_reason=reason` at liquidation value. Reasons: "reset", "settings changed",
  "completed" (`paper --hours N` without `--keep-running`). `paper_last_ended_run()` serves
  `/api/paper?run=previous`, so a reset at 23 h does not lose its verdict.
* `reset(now, start_capital, capital_source, target_hours)` = `end_run(now, "reset")` + `start`.
* `MemoryPaperPersistence` implements the protocol in memory (tests, backtests); TrackerStore
  implements it in SQL (E). Both must pass the same contract test (§7.8).

### 6.12 Backtest (`backtest.py`)

#### 6.12.1 Flow

`run_backtest(source, config, signal_fn=None, progress=None, clock=time.time)`:

1. `window = [start, end]` (config, else `end` = newest tick, `start = end - hours`);
   `pre = PreloadedHistory(source, start, end, warmup_s=config.warmup_s)` reads the whole window
   once (D52: one query per table and exchange; the dashboard passes a TrackerStore opened on a
   separate read-only connection, §7.1); `guard = GuardedHistory(pre)`;
   `market = HistoricalMarket(guard, config, pre)`; decision times `start, start + step_s, ..., <= end`.
2. Engine: `PaperEngine(config.paper with regime/params overrides applied, persistence=
   MemoryPaperPersistence())`; every portfolio's latency is `max(its own, config.latency_s)` (the
   human headline keeps 240 s); `max_fill_delay_s = max(paper.max_fill_delay_s, config.step_s +
   latency)` per portfolio; `interval_s = step_s`. Start capital = `config.paper.start_capital` or
   100,000.
3. For each `t`: `guard.set_time(t)`; `inputs = market.inputs(t)`; `obs = market.observation(t)`;
   `run_step(engine, inputs, obs, fulfil=market.fulfil, signal_fn)`. The same per-step read caps
   apply (no per-minute limiter). Stop early (`stopped_early`, a warning) when `time_budget_s` of
   wall time (`clock`) has been used.
4. Report: portfolios, equity curves, trades, verdicts (covered hours = window coverage), the
   event study, testability, coverage, assumptions, warnings.

**Performance acceptance** (D52): 24 h x 237 outcomes at `step_s` 300 (288 steps, ticks every 30 s)
finishes in under `PERFORMANCE_TARGET_S` (60 s) on a laptop: preloaded, bisect-indexed history; one
incremental `analytics._SurgeScanner` per exchange (prefix-safe: the decision at `t` reads only
prefix sums of points `<= t`; a test compares it with `detect_surges` on the series cut at `t`); no
per-step SQL. `tests/test_backtest.py` runs a scaled version always (40 outcomes, 6 h: under 10 s)
and the full size when `SUPERMARKET_PERF=1`.

#### 6.12.2 What the adapter may use at decision time `t`

* **Quotes**: the newest tick with `ts <= t` no older than `2 x step_s` (`source="tick"`); else, for
  single-outcome ideas only, if the newest candle closed at or before `t` (`close_ts <= t`) is no
  older than `CANDLE_QUOTE_MAX_AGE_S` (1 h), a synthetic quote `bid = floor_tick(close -
  assumed_spread / 2)`, `ask = ceil_tick(close + assumed_spread / 2)` with `source="candle"`; else no
  quote. **Set ideas (basket, arbitrage) need tick quotes of one snapshot for every leg** (§5.3,
  D53): candle quotes never form a set.
* **Books**: real `book_snapshots` with `ts <= t` (decisions: at most 900 s old) when
  `use_book_snapshots`; otherwise for fills only a **synthetic book**: one level at the quote's bid
  and one at its ask, each `min(assumed_touch_qty, volume_share x volume)` of the 1-h candles that
  **closed by the observation time** within `SYNTHETIC_DEPTH_CANDLE_S`, and **0 (no fill) when no such
  candle exists** (D22), `source="synthetic"`. Fill observations for pending orders: the first real
  snapshot (or the first tick, made into a synthetic book) with `ts >= after` and `<= t`. Synthetic
  books are **never** used for marks (marks use real snapshots or the depth-unknown rule, §6.7) and
  every fill from one is `synthetic` (left out of verdict statistics). The report states
  `SIZING_ASSUMPTION`: the backtest cannot tell sizing policies apart.
* **Tape for maker fills**: stored trades in `(since, t]` **fetched by `t`** (`fetched_at <= t`; rows
  with no `fetched_at`, from before schema v2, are not used, D25). If the exchange has none, **candle
  trade-throughs** (D21): only `5m` candles lying entirely inside `[created_at + latency,
  min(cancel_at, expires_at, t)]` (bucket start >= that start, `close_ts` <= that end) with `low < P`
  (YES bid) / `high > P` (YES ask); fill `min(remaining, candle_fill_cap, volume_share x volume x (P -
  low) / (high - low))` (YES bid; `(high - P) / (high - low)` for a YES ask) at the limit; the fill
  is `synthetic` and its reason says "candle fill (assumed)".
* **Surges**: re-detected by the per-exchange incremental scanner at `t` (`analytics._SurgeScanner`
  semantics, no look-ahead) and statuses via `update_surge_status` at `t`; an attribution is
  attached only from a stored surge (through `guard.surges(since, until=t)`) on the same exchange and
  direction whose `detected_at` lies within the detection's window and whose
  `attribution.analyzed_at <= t`. Never use a stored surge's later fields.
* **High bands**: `analytics.high_band(series <= t, t, ...)`.
* **Fair values** (`use_fair_values`, D24): per exchange the newest record with `ts <= t -
  latency_s` (availability), turned into a FairValue by `replay_fair_value(record, refresh, t,
  latency_s)`: its `as_of` is the record's live `as_of`, advanced to the oldest `fetched_at` of the
  record's venues in the newest `fair_value_refreshes` row with `ts <= t - latency` when that row is
  newer than the record and all those venues were "ok" in it (an unchanged value re-confirmed every
  minute stays fresh, as it was live); otherwise the value ages from the record's `as_of` and becomes
  stale after 90 s, as it would have live. Records with source "history" (imported, §4.11) are used
  when `use_history_fair_values`, aged from their bar end against `history_fv_max_age_s`, and marked
  `history=True`. Manual values are available from their record ts (never the user's `updated_at`).
  A test: a constant fair value recorded live (change-only records plus one refresh row per minute)
  produces a usable value at every replay step.
* **Settlements**: known at `max(settled_on, detected_at or settled_on) <= t` (D23).
* Leaderboard snapshots with `at <= t` (bar via `sizing.estimate_bar`); `infos` =
  `source.exchanges()` filtered to outcomes with a quote at `t`; `recent_mids` from the preloaded
  ticks in `(t - 300, t]`; constraints and overround rows: none (not stored).
* **`GuardedHistory`, per method** (D25): `series`, `candles`, `trades`, `book_snapshots`,
  `fair_value_history`, `fair_value_refreshes`, `leaderboard_snapshots` require `until <= t` (else
  `LookAheadError`, also when `until` is omitted) and post-filter what they return: candles with
  `close_ts > t` dropped (a partial bucket's high/low/close may cover the future), trades with `ts >
  t` or `fetched_at` None or `> t` dropped, records with `ts > t` dropped. `surges(since, until)`
  requires `until <= t`, drops rows with `detected_at > t`, returns copies with
  peak/status/current_price/reverted_fraction reset to their detection-time values and the
  attribution removed unless `analyzed_at <= t` (the store's `surges()` gains an `until` parameter
  and the guard asks with `limit` 100,000). `settlements()` filters by the availability rule above.
  `exchanges()` is allowed (metadata). `tick_bounds()` is allowed only before the first
  `set_time()`. `filtered` counts rows the post-filters dropped (coverage). Tests: a correct run
  never raises; a deliberately wrong adapter call raises; a **leaky partial candle** (1-h bucket
  starting at `t - 10 min` with a future high) and a **future surge** (detected after `t`, and an
  attribution analysed after `t`) are never visible at `t`.

#### 6.12.3 Assumptions, testability, coverage, sweeps

* **`testability`** (D26, shown first in the report and the UI): per trade kind `{"status",
  "hours", "sentence"}`:
  * `value`: "testable" when recorded or imported fair values cover the window, "partial", or
    "not_testable" ("value: not testable yet (0 h of outside prices recorded or imported in this
    window); run `fairvalue --import-history` or let the bot record for a day");
  * `basket`: hours of same-snapshot tick quotes ("basket: 14.0 h of tick quotes; candle-only
    hours cannot price a set");
  * `hole`, `fade`: hours with stored tape or real books vs candle-only;
  * `carry`: always "testable" (quotes only) but "pays at resolution: a one-day replay shows only
    its liquidation value";
  * `arbitrage`: always "not_replayable" (`NOT_REPLAYABLE_ARBITRAGE`, D39).
* `assumptions` always lists, with the configured numbers: the decision grid and latency (and that
  the headline portfolio waits 240 s); how quotes, depth and passive fills were synthesised where
  history lacked them; `SIZING_ASSUMPTION`; that fair values replay only what was recorded or
  imported (imported Polymarket history is indicative, not executable); that the tape is sparse
  (the tracker stores trades only for surging outcomes and paper orders); that attribution verdicts
  are used only once analysed; `NOT_REPLAYABLE_ARBITRAGE`.
* `coverage`: exchanges, decision steps, and the share of (exchange, step) pairs whose quote came
  from ticks vs candles, of fills from real books vs synthetic books, of steps with a usable fair
  value (recorded vs imported), of maker fills from the tape vs candles, of set ideas priced from
  same-snapshot ticks, and `guard_filtered`.
* `warnings`: "Only N hours of data" (< 12 h); "Most quotes are candle-based: spreads and depth are
  assumptions" (candle share > 50%); "No fair values were recorded in this window: value ideas could
  not trade"; **`OVERLAP_WARNING`** when the window overlaps the live paper run
  (`config.live_run_started_at`; `overlap_hours` in the report: "... a backtest over the same data
  is not an independent check, so agreement between the two is not evidence"); `SWEEP_WARNING` for
  sweeps; `stopped_early`.
* `parse_sweep(["min_value_edge=0.01,0.02", "latency_s=30,60"])`; values parsed as int, float,
  bool or string; names looked up in `StrategyParams`, then `BacktestConfig`, then
  `PaperConfig`; unknown name -> ValueError listing the valid ones.
* `sweep(source, base, grid)`: Cartesian product, at most 50 runs (else ValueError); returns the
  base run's report with `sweep` rows `{"params": {...}, "label": "min_value_edge=0.01,
  latency_s=30", "pnl_liq", "trades_closed", "verdict_level", "max_drawdown"}` for the
  headline portfolio, sorted by `pnl_liq` desc, and `SWEEP_WARNING` in `warnings`. The CLI's
  `--latency-sweep` is `latency_s=30,120,300` (D54): how much of the result survives acting later.

### 6.13 Execution statistics and "why no trades" (D7, D62)

Per portfolio and kind, `PortfolioSummary.execution[kind]`:
`{"entries": orders, "filled": orders with any fill, "fill_rate", "avg_slippage": mean of
fill.slippage over filled entries (contract price; positive = worse than the decision-time touch),
"unfilled": entries with no fill, "unfilled_pnl_now": sum over the tracked unfilled entries of qty x
(today's contract bid - decision_touch) ("what the signals that did NOT fill would show now if they
had filled at the decision-time price"), "exits", "avg_exit_slippage": mean of planned_price - exit
price}`. This makes the selection effect of immediate-or-cancel visible: for fades and momentum the
dropped trades are usually the losers, for value usually the winners.

`PortfolioSummary.no_trade_reason` (when the portfolio has no fills), from per-kind counters
(steps, steps with a signal of the kind, signals, entered, blocked by reason, steps with a usable
fair value):
* "No value trades yet: usable outside fair values on 0% of steps (see the fair-value status)."
* "No fade signals in 18.0 h: the market offered no such setup (that is not evidence of no edge)."
* "12 basket signals, none entered: waiting for fresh books of every leg (budget)."

### 6.14 Signal-level event study (`study.py`, D6)

One day of closed trades cannot reach significance (mean +100, sd 250 per trade needs about 17
independent trades; with intra-day correlation 0.3, 20 trades are worth about 3), and the main
profit sources (holding value to resolution, election night, the end regime) are not in a one-day
window at all. The event study measures what one day CAN measure:

* Every `value` and `basket` signal opens an event at its first appearance in an episode (a new
  `idea_id`, or one absent for `EPISODE_GAP_S` (30 min)), whether or not any portfolio traded it.
  `entry` = the decision-time contract ask (sets: the sum over legs) from `obs.quotes`.
* At each horizon (+5 min, +30 min, +2 h, +6 h) the first bulk quote at or after `t0 + h` (within
  `2 x interval`) gives the outcome: value `contract bid - entry` per share; NO basket `sum(1 - YES
  ask_i) - entry`; YES basket `sum(YES bid_i) - entry` (net of the spread by construction).
  `converged`: value, the Cup contract mid moved at least half the original gap toward the t0 fair
  value; basket, the set's touch edge fell below half its entry value. `reversed` (value): the
  outside fair value moved toward the Cup's t0 mid by at least half the gap (the gap "closed"
  because our fair value was stale, D18).
* `summary()`: per kind and horizon `n`, mean, a two-way cluster t-interval (races x entry hour,
  90%, needs 20 events and 10 clusters), share positive, share converged, share reversed; plus the
  fixed texts `CAN_SHOW` and `CANNOT_SHOW` (§11), shown next to the portfolio verdict.
* Uses only bulk quotes and fair values already in each observation (no extra reads); runs in the
  backtest too. Completed events are persisted (`paper_put_events`), pending ones live in the state.

### 6.15 Concurrency (D43)

* `PaperRunner` owns one `threading.RLock`; `step`, `reset` and `end_run` take it (the tracker's
  `paper_step()`, its worker and the POST reset all go through the runner). The engine is not
  thread-safe and is only touched under that lock.
* At the end of every step and after every reset/end, under the lock, the runner builds the full
  `/api/paper` body once (portfolios, verdicts, study summary, equity downsampled to 300 points,
  budget, `extras_fn()` output) and publishes it by assigning one attribute. `summary()` returns the
  published dict without the lock; callers never mutate it (web serialises it; the tracker copies
  before adding keys).
* Lock order: runner lock -> FairValueService lock -> tracker lock. `inputs_fn` (which calls
  `tracker.view()`) and `extras_fn` run under the runner lock and take the others briefly. The
  tracker never calls the runner, the engine or the FairValueService while holding `tracker._lock`
  (it collects what it needs, releases, then calls).

  `room() <= 0`, then takes the shared slot. Backfill and analysis limiters become gated the same

  it `analyze_limiter`). The tracker-loop's own reads (`self._bot`, main client with retries) are
  never gated. Result: background reads defer while 78 of the 90 client slots are used.

## 9. Read budget (Super Market: 100 reads/min per account across all keys, client cap 90/min)

Live Cup: 237 binary markets, 237 outcomes, 30-s interval. The tracker computes the projection
below from the live outcome count `n` at start-up and after each market-list refresh and shows it
in `/api/status` (`read_budget.projected_per_min`, warning above 75, D44).

| consumer | reads | per minute |
|---|---|---|
| bulk prices | `ceil(n/100)` = 3 per cycle | 6.0 |
| market list | 3 per 300 s; early re-reads while outcomes settle (`MISSING_REFRESH_MIN_S` 30 s x 3 pages) | 0.6 (up to 6 near a settlement) |
| context | constraints 1 + tournament 1 + pnl 1 + leaderboard(100) 1 + idea books <= 12 (+ overround: 0, the Cup has no multi-outcome markets) per 300 s | <= 3.2 |
| settlements | `ceil(settled/100)` per 600 s (usually 1) | 0.1-0.3 |
| backfill | `2n` = 474 reads at <= 30/min in the first ~16 min, then 0 (n = 470 would be 940 reads, ~32 min) | 30 -> 0 |
| analysis | ~2 per new or grown surge, limiter 20/min | ~2 (bursts <= 20) |
| dashboard drawers | 1 book per open drawer per 15 s | 4 per open drawer |
| **paper** | <= 8 books + 2 tape pages per step; 20/min, **6/min while the backfill queue is pending** | **<= 20 (6)** |
| main-client retries | up to 4 retries per bulk read during a 5xx burst (3 reads x 4 retries x 2 cycles) | up to +24 for a minute |
| **total, steady** | | **~36-40** |
| **total, worst case at start-up** | 6 + 6 + 3.2 + 0.3 + 30 + 20 + 8 (two drawers) + 6 (paper during backfill) | **~80, plus retries** |

The client caps everything at 90/min; the **reserve** keeps 12 of those for the snapshot loop:
every background read waits (backfill, analysis, context) or is skipped (paper) while 78 or more
are in use, so a burst of retries or a busy start-up delays the optional work, never the
snapshots. The account limit is 100/min across **all** keys: running a second copy of the bot (or
another script) on the same account would exceed it; the lease refuses a second tracker on the
same store, and the README says "one process per account" (another script gets about 10/min).

Outside hosts (not part of the Super Market budget): Polymarket ~5 GET/min (+ <= 3 discovery
calls per 6 h), Kalshi ~3 GET/min; each host limited to 30/min by its provider; a refresh stops
starting requests after 30 s. Demo (real time): client 600/min, backfill 240, analysis 120, paper
60. Demo fast mode: client 10,000, backfill 10,000, analysis 10,000, paper 600 per simulated minute.

## 11. Honesty texts (exact, used by C, E and F)

`paper.CAVEATS` (always shown with any simulation result; `VERDICT_CAVEATS` = items 1-4, shown
next to the verdict):

1. "A day is a small sample, and trades on different races share one national polling factor, so they are not independent: treat any result as a hint, not proof."
2. "Value and carry ideas pay mostly when races are decided (November 3-4). A one-day test shows them at liquidation value, spread included, so it favours ideas that converge fast and understates ideas meant to be held to resolution."
3. "You will act by hand, minutes after a signal, against bots that react in seconds. The headline portfolio waits about 4 minutes before it can fill; the bot-speed portfolios (30 s) are an upper bound for manual copying, especially for baskets and liquidity holes."
4. "Simulated orders have no market impact: a real order of the same size would move the price against you and could be seen by other bots."
5. "Fills are simulated from order books read after each decision; real fills can be worse (other bots get there first, queue position) or occasionally better."
6. "Profit is shown at liquidation value: what selling every position into the bids you could see would fetch. Marks at the mid and the model's own valuation at fair value are higher and are shown only for reference."
7. "The Cup's end-of-tournament settlement rule is unknown, so positions still open at the end are valued conservatively rather than at 1.00."
8. "Portfolios are separate what-ifs that run on the same signals; they do not compete with each other for the same liquidity."
9. "Nothing was traded: the bot is read-only and every order here is simulated."

`paper.DEMO_CAVEAT`: "Demo data: the market is simulated and its mispricings are scripted (the
demo's outside prices lead the Cup by design), so these results say nothing about real
profitability."

`paper.TABLE_WARNING`: "The best of {n} portfolios looks better than it is by chance: judge the
headline, and confirm any single kind on data recorded later."

`paper.FV_MODEL_LABEL`: "The model's own opinion of these positions (valued at the outside fair
value that generated them): not evidence of profit."

`study.CAN_SHOW`: "What one day can show: how often, and how fast, Cup prices move toward the
outside fair value after a signal, net of the spread, over hundreds of signals."
`study.CANNOT_SHOW`: "What it cannot show: whether the outside fair value is right about who wins
(that pays only when races are decided, November 3-4), how prices behave on election night, or how
the Cup's end-of-tournament settlement will work."

Verdict sentences: §6.9. Fair-value caveats: §10.4. Backtest: `SWEEP_WARNING`,
`OVERLAP_WARNING`, `SIZING_ASSUMPTION`, `NOT_REPLAYABLE_ARBITRAGE` (backtest.py) and §6.12.3.

## 13. Known unknowns (do not resolve them by guessing in code)

* The Cup's end-settlement regime (`resolved_outcomes` vs a 5-hour VWAP closeout): exposed as
  the `--regime` option, default `unknown` (conservative). The uncalled-branch model of §5.2 is a
  documented heuristic.
* Whether D/R pairs are linked by ALL relationships (collateral savings): `--all-collateral`
  is off by default.
* Each market's resolution source (media call vs certification): `called_prob` is a documented
  heuristic.
* How the mid-Cup leaderboard values are computed: the bar estimate says they are marks.
* How fast the user really acts: the headline assumes 4 minutes (`HUMAN_LATENCY_S`); the backtest's
  `--latency-sweep` shows the sensitivity.
* The national-factor parameters (`race_margin_sd_pts` 7, `national_swing_sd_pts` 3, `FACTOR_RHO`
  0.5) are round, documented assumptions, not estimates.
* Live Polymarket/Kalshi reachability: this sandbox cannot reach them; the user's machine can.
  The code must degrade to "no outside fair value" and say so.
* Pins come from other participants' public files: validated live before use (two were provably
  wrong and are blanked, §4.5).

### 14.2 Statistics and the verdict

**D1. Count every idea entered, not only closed trades** (R1-2, R2-18). Value positions exit
winners at a target and have no stop, so in one day the losers stay open: 5 converged ideas
(+250 each, closed) and 5 drifting ones (-200 each, open) showed a closed-trade mean of +250 and a
5/5 win rate. The statistic is now over `IdeaOutcome`s: closed at realised P&L, open at
liquidation P&L; closed and open are reported separately; the win rate is labelled "closed trades
only: biased toward quick winners". §6.9.

**D2. Two-way cluster-robust t-interval, G - 1 degrees of freedom, stricter thresholds** (R1-1).
Clusters: races, and 2-hour blocks of the entry time x party direction; `G = min` of the two
counts; `SE` from the Cameron-Gelbach-Miller two-way formula with the `G/(G-1)` correction;
`t_quantile` (stdlib incomplete beta) with `G - 1` df. Thresholds: 12 covered hours, 30 ideas,
G >= 20. Simulated at zero edge with balanced directions the lower bound exceeds 0 in 4.0-5.5% of
runs (nominal 5%); revision 1's bootstrap gave 7-8% at 8 races and up to 26% with a day factor.
§6.9.

**D3. One day can at most be "promising, not proof"** (R1-1). No within-day interval can see the
day's own national shock: with 80% of ideas leaning one party the zero-edge false-positive rate
is 11-14%. New level `promising` (the strongest one-day wording; the sentence says to confirm on a
second day before acting); `positive` needs 48 covered hours on two UTC days with >= 6 h each and a
positive result on each of the last two days (false positives back to 4.5-5.3% at shocks up to
0.25x the per-idea noise). `Verdict.level` gains the value "promising" (`VERDICT_LEVELS`). §6.9.

**D4. One pre-registered headline; everything else exploratory** (R1-1, R2-16). Nine portfolios at
a nominal 5% each would make "the best one is profitable" wrong about a third of the time. The
headline is fixed before the run: the user's own sizing policy at human speed (`human:<sizing>`,
D7), shown first. Every other portfolio is exploratory: judged at `1 - 0.10 / n_portfolios`,
capped at "promising", badge "Exploratory: ...". The UI never names or highlights a best portfolio;
the table carries `TABLE_WARNING`. §6.1, §6.9, §8.2.

**D5. Guards against one-off results** (R1-30, R2-18). `pnl_ex_best` removes the best idea, open or
closed; a second interval on the per-idea return on capital (so an 8,000-set basket does not
dominate a 50-share hole); downgrades to "inconclusive" when one race supplies over 50% of the P&L,
when a one-sd (3-point) national swing would move equity by over 10%, or when over 20% of equity is
marked without a recent book; frozen and synthetic ideas are left out and named. §6.9.

**D6. A signal-level event study** (R1-3). One day of closed trades rarely reaches significance
(mean +100, sd 250 needs ~17 independent trades; with intra-day correlation 0.3, 20 trades are worth
~3), and it cannot see hold-to-resolution value, election night or the end regime at all. `study.py`
records every value and basket signal, traded or not, at +5 min / +30 min / +2 h / +6 h, net of the
spread, with "converged" and "reversed" shares and a two-way clustered interval: hundreds of
observations a day, shown next to the verdict with fixed "can show / cannot show" sentences. §6.14.

### 14.3 Execution realism

**D7. Judge the user's speed: a human-latency headline and execution statistics** (R1-4, R2-17).
A 30-s automated executor's result does not transfer to a person acting minutes later on gaps
that are raced away within minutes. The headline portfolio `human:<sizing>` fills only from a book
observed at least 240 s after the decision (`HUMAN_LATENCY_S`, max fill delay 360 s); bot-speed
portfolios remain as an upper bound. Per kind: fill rate, slippage vs the decision-time touch,
exit slippage, and what the signals that did NOT fill would show now (immediate-or-cancel drops
the losers of fades and the winners of value ideas; now visible). The backtest offers
`--latency-sweep` (30/120/300 s). §6.1, §6.13, §6.12.3.

**D8. Buy limits floor-ticked; Kelly on the depth-walked average fill** (R1-5). `ceil_tick` gave
away more than half the edge (0.5466 -> 0.55 kept 0.026 of a 0.053 edge; basket legs kept 0.02 of
0.05). Buy limits now use `floor_tick` (sell limits `ceil_tick`); value limits are lowered tick by
tick until the MARGINAL edge clears the full required edge (0.545 -> 0.535 in the hand example);
sizes come from `depth_kelly_units` on the expected average fill (5,326 shares at the touch ->
4,601 on the example book); unknown depth: Kelly at the touch, and the chaser at most half-Kelly
(its fills may walk to the limit). §5.1, §5.2, §5.6.1.

**D19. Maker fills need a known queue and the right taker side** (R1-13). A hole order 46 ticks
under the bid lies beyond a 20-level book, so its queue read as 0 and every at-price print filled
50%. `queue_ahead` is now None (only trade-throughs fill) when the price lies beyond the last
displayed level or the book is over 120 s old; it is decremented across prints; an at-price print
counts only when its taker hit our side. §6.4.

**D23. Settlements when they were known, and before fills** (R1-16). Replays use settlements at
`max(settled_on, detected_at)` (settledOn can be backdated: a YES bought at 0.60 was paid +0.40 from
10:00 although detected at 16:00); the engine runs settlements before fills and never fills on an
outcome that left the open list. §6.2, §6.6, §6.12.2.

**D29. Basket exits on depth-walked proceeds; legging out is closed out** (R1-21). The exit rule
fires on the touch but executes only if the depth-walked proceeds of the whole set quantity clear
the profit; if one leg sells and another does not within the fill delay, the rest is force-sold
("legging"), so a "riskless" set never silently becomes a naked NO. §6.5.

**D30. Set entries wait for fresh books of every leg** (R1-22). Blind entries sized at the riskless
cap (8,421 sets) created large legging residues sold at 0.005, losses unrelated to the edge that
made `kind:basket` look bad. A set idea is deferred until every leg has a real book at most 60 s
old (one step), units are capped by the thinner leg, and the read plan reads a group's books all
together or not at all. §6.2, §6.3, §6.10.

**D31. Marks never price a large position entirely at the touch** (R1-23, R2-5). Without a fresh
consistent book, a position is marked from the last real book (at most 1 h old) shifted to today's
touch ("stale"), else 100 shares at the touch and the rest at half of it ("unknown"); synthetic
books never mark; mark reads go largest position first; the depth-unknown and stale shares of
equity are reported, and over 20% unknown caps the verdict at inconclusive. §6.7, §6.10.

**D34. The exit tick is charged** (R1-27). `convergence_gain = target_bid - 0.005 - entry` (the exit
order's limit); exit slippage vs the planned price is reported per kind. §5.2, §6.13.

**D35. Taken liquidity stays taken as long as the cooldown** (R1-28). `consumed_memory_s` = 1800 s
(>= `reentry_cooldown_s`), matched within one tick, so a re-entering idea cannot take the same
house rung again, re-quoted or not. §6.3.

**D40. Tape truncation is paged and flagged** (R1-35, R2-19). The reader follows the cursor up to 3
pages within the step budget; a last page still full marks the read truncated, sets
`PaperOrder.tape_gap` and counts in `StepReport.tape_gaps`. §6.4, §6.10, §7.2.

**D45. Frozen positions count 0 and are named** (R1-29). A market closing without a ruling often
follows news, so its last mark can hide a loss. Frozen positions are "unvalued": 0 in `equity_liq`
and `pnl_liq`, their last value shown separately, left out of the verdict and named in its
sentence. §6.6.

### 14.4 Strategy and sizing

**D9. Certainty shrink** (R1-6, R1-11). Kelly on an estimated edge overbets; `k_eff = k x edge^2 /
(edge^2 + sigma^2)` with sigma = the fair value's uncertainty (or a kind default), for value ideas in
both policies and every directional chaser idea. §5.6.1.

**D10. The national-swing measure is delta-weighted; set legs are excluded** (R1-6, R1-25). Cost
tilt charged a 0.95 safe seat 0.95 per share although it barely moves with a national swing, and
counted riskless basket legs (+1,263 for 8,421 sets). `factor_delta = phi(Phi^-1(p)) / 7` per share
per point (0.057 at a toss-up, 0.015 at 0.95); `national_tilt_d = sum(qty x delta)`; the conservative
cap: a 3-point national miss may cost at most 10% of equity (about the old cap at toss-ups). §5.1,
§5.6.1.

**D11. A correlated slate is sized as one factor bet** (R1-6). Five D-leaning toss-ups at ~11% each
put ~55% of equity on one factor (2-3x Kelly on it). Each same-direction directional idea gets
`1 / (1 + (n_same - 1) x 0.5)` of its own Kelly (5 ideas -> 1/3 each, ~18% in all), and the chaser
keeps a swing cap outside the swing window (40% chase, 30% near/unknown). "Concentrate" now means
one large factor bet, not several full-Kelly bets. §5.6.4.

**D12. top_k counts what is already held** (R1-7). Each step used to add 5 more directional
positions. Slots = `top_k - (open + pending directional)`; a new idea replaces the weakest held one
only if its score is 50% higher (the engine sells that one first). §5.6.4.

**D13. At most one bold bet, and only if it can reach the bar** (R1-8). With the 60% cap the win
state reaches the 221,500 bar only for costs <= 0.3306 (at 0.45 it wins only to 173,333, wasting the
variance). The chaser makes one bold bet, on the best idea that passes that test; otherwise it sizes
as chase and says why; never two at once (their win states are not joint). §5.6.4.

**D14. The bar is estimated linearly from the lower end** (R1-9). Compounding a clipped 5%/day over
30 days multiplied the bar by 4.3, and 12 h of history turned one 2% mark move into M = 7.6. Growth
is now linear, the median over ranks 3-10, from at least 3 days of history, clipped at 2%/day, and
only shown (range low-high); sizing uses `low = max(third, floor)`. §5.6.3.

**D15. Mode hysteresis** (R1-9, R1-36). Switching across M = 1.3 or 10 needs M 10% beyond the
threshold and at least 6 h in the current mode, so 99k vs 100k equity no longer flips full Kelly to
conservative. §5.6.4.

**D16. The uncalled branch of a VWAP closeout can lose** (R1-10). An uncalled race is a close race:
with margin N(D+1, 4) and "called if |m| > 2", P(D | uncalled) = 0.531 although P(D) = 0.599. The
uncalled branch now closes at `E[p | uncalled] = 0.5 + (q - 0.5) x 0.3` minus the persisting Cup
mispricing, the called branch at `q_c` so the two average to q; a 0.97 favourite's 3% uncalled
branch contributes -0.0101 per share, about its whole carry gap. Labelled a heuristic; positions
relying on it are flat before the closeout window. §5.2, §5.5.

**D17. Trade only gaps larger than the fair value's own uncertainty; NEAR matches display-only**
(R1-11, R2-23). Required edge = `min_value_edge + uncertainty` (max of venue disagreement, the
tightest half-spread and the venue fee band; x2 on the minimum below q = 0.15); NEAR matches
(chamber control: "win the U.S. Senate" vs "control after the midterms" vs the pro tempore's party)
are shown but not traded unless the user sets `trade_near`, then with +0.03 more edge. §4.7, §5.2.

**D18. Fresh fair values only, and no buying into a stale gap** (R1-12). Max age 90 s (refresh 60 s);
skip when the outside value jumped by more than half the gap between refreshes, or when the Cup moved
away from the fair value since it was read by more than half the gap (faster bots anchored to
Polymarket already moved it); the event study measures how often gaps "reverse" (close by the outside
price moving) rather than converge. §5.2, §6.14.

**D27. Baskets are riskless only when they are** (R1-20). A refund on one leg (bids 0.60/0.45: D
refunded, R wins -> 0.40 back for 0.95) or an uncalled race under a VWAP closeout breaks the floor.
`riskless` only under `resolved_outcomes` or fast-count races; otherwise a `bounded` bet (refund
tail 1%, loss = cost - cheapest leg); chaser sets capped at 20% (was 50%); floor and liquidation
value shown side by side. §5.3, §5.6.

**D32. Count a basket's edge once; state Markov correctly** (R1-24). Set legs carry `edge / n`;
the sentence now reads "A strategy whose expected multiple is 1.04x reaches 2.2x with probability at
most 47%; with no edge (fair prices) the bound is 1/M = 45%." §5.6.3, §6.8.

**D33. Rank by expected growth, not return per dollar** (R1-26). `score = growth_score`: expected
profit in % of equity at the conservative capped size x confidence (toss-up +0.05: 0.25; longshot
0.10 +0.02: 0.11; the old score ranked them 0.10 vs 0.20); plus the longshot edge multiplier. §5.1.

**D36. Fades aim no further than fair value; p is measured** (R1-31). Target = the nearer of
half-reversion and the fair value less half the spread; no fade under a 0.02 gain after spreads; p
capped by the measured net-of-spread reversion rate of participant-driven surges (n >= 10), the
arbitrary floor of 0.65 removed. §5.5.

**D38. Fair values are consistent within a race** (R1-33, R2-23). After blending and combining,
legs of a fully valued race are renormalised to sum to 1 (or all made unusable when they disagree);
a placeholder leg dropped by the provider is not "quoted" and does not poison the race sum; two value
ideas on different winners of one race keep only the better one. §4.7, §5.1.

### 14.5 Backtest

**D21. Candle fills only from small candles inside the order's life** (R1-14). A 1-h bucket's
volume was mostly at other prices, and lows after a cancel still filled. Only 5m candles entirely
inside `[created + latency, min(cancel_at, expires_at, t)]`; `qty = min(remaining, 50, 10% x volume x
the share of the range below the price)`; flagged synthetic and excluded from verdict statistics.
§6.12.2.

**D22. Synthetic books fill only, never mark, and have no depth without a candle** (R1-15). They
gave the least liquid outcomes the most depth and made marks swing. Depth comes only from candles
closed by the observation time (0 without one); marks use real books or the D31 rule; the report
says the backtest cannot tell sizing policies apart. §6.12.2.

**D24. Fair-value replay matches what was live** (R1-17, R2-12). Records on a one-tick change plus
a 10-minute heartbeat, with their live `as_of`; one `fair_value_refreshes` row per refresh proves
re-confirmation, so an unchanged value stays fresh in the replay exactly while it was fresh live
(revision 1 suppressed ~60% of value ideas and made some look younger than they were); manual values
are available from their record time, never `updated_at`. §4.8, §6.12.2.

**D25. The look-ahead guard filters what it returns, per method** (R1-18, R2-24). Partial candles
(`close_ts > t`), surges detected or analysed after `t`, tape fetched after `t` (new
`trades.fetched_at`), later surge fields and `tick_bounds` after the first `set_time` are all
filtered or refused; `surges()` gains `until`; tests use a deliberately leaky candle and a future
surge. §6.12.2, §7.1.

**D26. Say what can be tested; allow an outside-history import** (R1-19, R2-15). The report opens
with a per-kind testability table ("value: not testable yet (0 h of outside prices ...)"); an
opt-in, GET-only import of Polymarket `prices-history` (indicative) and Kalshi candlesticks (bid/ask,
bar end <= t - latency) lets value be replayed over the Cup's first days; a window overlapping the
live run carries `OVERLAP_WARNING` (agreement is not evidence). §4.11, §6.12.3.

**D39. Arbitrage is "not replayable"** (R1-34): constraints and overround rows are not stored; the
testability table and the assumptions say so. §6.12.3.

**D52. Backtests do not compete with the live loop** (R2-13). History is preloaded once per window
(no per-step SQL, no 200 M point reads), surges come from one incremental scanner per exchange, the
dashboard runs one backtest at a time on a separate read-only connection with a 120-s budget and
yields between steps; acceptance: 24 h x 237 outcomes at 300 s under 60 s, and `run_once` latency
within 2x while a backtest runs. §6.12.1, §7.4.

**D53. Set ideas only from same-snapshot ticks** (R2-14). Candle closes from different times made
fake baskets (0.60 fifty minutes ago + 0.47 now = 1.07); every leg now needs a tick quote of one bulk
snapshot (|dts| <= 5 s), live and in replays; coverage reports the share. §5.3, §6.12.2.

### 14.6 Engineering

**D20. Read-only enforced at runtime and checked by an AST test** (R2-3). `readonly.py`
